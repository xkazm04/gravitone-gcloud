# Script patterns — trailer (90–150s)

Read [`CRAFT-BASELINE.md`](../../../../CRAFT-BASELINE.md) and
[`ENGINES.md`](../../../../ENGINES.md) first. **This file owns the full spine**, because this is the
rung the others are derived from — the sibling templates cover subtraction
([`teaser`](../../../teaser/steps/01-script/PATTERNS.md)) and a different production stage
([`cinematic`](../../../cinematic/steps/01-script/PATTERNS.md)), and neither restates what is here.

**Sources:** n=0. Nothing torn down, timed or counted in this repo. Every rule below is doctrine
written elsewhere, cited by name. See the template's evidence gap.

---

## 1. The product is an unpaid debt, and every neighbouring rule inverts

Stated once, at the top, because it is what makes everything below read correctly:

> "A trailer is a piece of narrative whose product is an **unpaid debt**. Every other short form in
> this bundle exists to deliver something […] A trailer exists to make somebody watch a different
> artifact later, and it succeeds by opening a gap it deliberately does not close."

The three educational templates in this library obey the opposite rule, and the registry names the
boundary in both directions: their test that a withheld answer "reads as not having one" is "correct
there and **inverted here**", and this subject's rule that the peak need not be the work's peak "is
meaningless there."

**What does not invert:** but/therefore beat linking and act-as-question architecture. The registry
applies them "unchanged" and deliberately does not restate them — an escalation whose rungs link only
with "and then" is flat for exactly the reason `CRAFT-BASELINE.md` gives.

## 2. The spine, and the dip that is the whole shape

Four parts and an optional button. **Two practitioner communities converge on it from opposite
directions** — picture editors describe cold open / introduction / escalation / climax; cue writers
describe four movements plus a five-second tail for title and logos — and the registry's reading of
that convergence is the load-bearing part:

> "They agree because they are describing one object: **the cue is the skeleton, and the picture parts
> are named after the cue movements they sit on.**"

| Part | What it is FOR | Its test |
|---|---|---|
| **Cold open** | buys attention with something that needs no context | does the viewer want to know how the moment resolves **and** believe it will resolve soon? Optional — a work with an existing audience is better off without one |
| **Introduction** | "the least information that makes the stakes legible" | this is "the part that gets cut first at shorter lengths, and the part whose absence turns the escalation into noise" |
| **Escalation** | raises a variable across rungs | each rung "closes one piece of information and opens the next" |
| **Climax** | the peak — and "emphatically *not required to be the work's own climax*" | a cut ending on the third act's turn "has spent the artifact it was selling" |
| **Button** | optional last joke, sting or flourish | **one hard constraint: smaller than the climax.** "A button that outperforms the peak retroactively re-reads the peak as its setup" |

**The shape is not a ramp.** The naive reading — best moments, ordered by size, biggest last — is what
the whole form is organised against:

> "Practitioners describe the correct shape as almost the opposite of a ramp — open strong to arrest
> attention, then *fall back* to a quiet setup, and build from there to a peak. **The dip is not a
> lull. It is where the debt gets opened.**"

Vault **C1** (confidence High) converges from four independent sources, and S1 states the same fact as
an energy rule: *"If a trailer feels high energy all the time, then none of it will feel high energy."*

## 3. The escalation looks like a reversal chain and is not one

This is the section most likely to be got wrong by anyone carrying craft over from the educational
templates. Quoted:

> "In a self-sufficient piece a turn *explains* — it settles an expectation, signals the break, and
> then delivers the **mechanism** […] A trailer cannot do that, **because the mechanism is the thing
> it is selling.** So its turns raise **stakes** instead."

Remove the mechanism and the naive substitute is size — bigger, louder, faster — which produces "a
montage where the content varies and the magnitude does not, which a viewer experiences as **one long
beat repeated**."

**What actually replaces the mechanism is closure.** Practitioners describe the rungs as *chapter
breaks*: "a progression should happen after you have finished giving something, not in the middle of
giving it."

A rung has three properties, and none of them is a shot:

- **It closes.** "A rung that trails into the next one is not a rung; it is the same rung, longer."
- **It links.** "If the only honest connector is *and then*, the chain is a list."
- **It raises exactly one variable.** "A rung that raises three at once has nothing left for the rung
  after it."

The four moves that raise stakes while withholding the explanation, **in the source's order of how
much they cost**:

1. **Widen the scope** — same threat, more affected. "Cheapest, and the first to become boring if used
   twice in a row."
2. **Shorten the clock** — "strong because it needs no new information at all".
3. **Personalise the cost** — abstraction → a named person met in the introduction. "This is the move
   that requires the introduction to have happened, and it is **why cutting the introduction hollows
   out the escalation**."
4. **Invert the frame** — the rug pull. "The only move that borrows from the work's turn, and it must
   borrow *that the frame is wrong* without borrowing *what the frame is* — otherwise it is a spend,
   not an escalation."

**Rung floor:** "Below roughly ten seconds per rung the closure disappears and the chain reads as one
montage; a short cut should carry two or three rungs, not six compressed ones." At 90–150s that is
comfortably three to five.

Vault **C6** (Medium-High) is move 4 seen from the editing room, and its vocabulary is worth keeping:
*rug pull, bumper, rise, signature sound (leitmotif), diegetic* — "a distinct tone shift, usually going
from lighthearted to something more unsettling."

## 4. The reset is what makes a climax legible as one

> "The deliberate cessation of the cue — a stop, a fall to near-silence, sometimes to black […] It is
> the only true silence the form has, and **the single device that separates a trailer with a climax
> from a trailer that is merely loud for two minutes and then stops.**"

The mechanism is arithmetic, not taste: "a peak is perceived against the level it rose from, so a cut
that never falls has no headroom left to peak into."

**Four constraints, and violating any one makes it read as a mistake:**

1. **Stop on a natural downbeat** — "a cue cut mid-phrase reads as a dropout".
2. **Put a visual on the final beat** — "silence arriving over a continuing shot reads as a technical
   fault".
3. **Sweeten the stop** — an added accent "makes the cessation deliberate. Without it the ear hears
   something break."
4. **Fill the silence with one thing** — "a reset that holds two ideas has spent its whole value
   carrying neither."

**What the silence holds** decides what the reset means: *a line* (the form's substitute for
narration — "what was *said* is now *punctuated*"), *an image* (a held detail, an intake of breath), or
*nothing at all* ("the strongest and the most fragile").

**Exactly one.** "When there are three or more, remove all but one. Repetition converts the device from
a reset into a rhythm." A punchline reset may coexist with the structural one "if the first is brief
and clearly smaller".

And the warning that matters for a tool: "**Do not reset into a peak the escalation has not earned.**
The device makes whatever follows sound like an arrival, which means it will faithfully announce an
arrival that is not there."

## 5. The honesty problem lives in the assembly

Dialogue in this form is **cobbled** — "sub-clipped from anywhere in the work, indexed by speaker and
text, and reassembled into a sequence the work never utters". The technique is legitimate and it is
"how the form achieves density". It is "also a machine for manufacturing implications out of true
parts":

> "A question cut against an unrelated answer asserts that the answer belongs to it. A reaction cut
> against a reveal asserts the character saw it. A line cut against a face asserts it was said to
> them. **None of these are lies at the shot level and all of them are claims at the sequence level,
> so audit the sequence.**"

Promises come from three places and only the first is obvious: **by claim** (auditable, it is text),
**by register** (cue, grade, cutting rhythm — "a cut that promises comedy in its register and delivers
a bleak work has made a false promise without a single false statement"), and **by assembly**.

The pass that catches it has one hard precondition:

> "Watch the cut once as a stranger and write down everything you now believe about the work […] **Do
> not consult the work while doing this** — the whole value of the pass is that it is done from
> ignorance, and **anyone who knows the work cannot perform it.**"

Both directions are the same sin, and the quiet one is worse understood: "Overpromising is the loud
one. **Underselling is the quiet one**, and it is what a cut does when it advertises a safe, legible
version of a strange work in order to test well."

**The tooling rule, quoted, because it is a product decision and not a craft one:** "In a tool, make
the payer a required field. If a system composes or checks promotional cuts, a promise-bearing beat
that carries no reference to the moment paying it should be surfaced as incomplete rather than
accepted." — and its limit, in the same document: "**As a gate.** Every grade in the ledger is a
judgement […] A system that blocks on it will block on correct work; report the rows and let a human
read them."

## 6. The cue is the parent, and this studio has the dependency backwards

> "Editors select the music first and lay the cue out as the framework before any footage goes down;
> **the acts of the finished cut *are* the movements of the cue.** This is not a stylistic preference —
> it is the dependency order of the form."

A cue is chosen for its **edit affordances**, not how it sounds: "hits, fills, breaths, and a beat of
silence between sections. A beautiful, continuous, evenly-textured piece of music is close to unusable
here, because it offers no boundaries to hang structure on."

Three consequences the registry draws, all of them planning-level:

- "**Act boundaries are cue boundaries.** Planning act durations in the abstract and then looking for
  music that fits produces a search with no results."
- "**The reset has a position, not a preference.**"
- "**Shortening preserves the cue's shape, not the picture's.**"

Vault **C9** (High) says the same thing from three independent directions — S5 (Paul Cartlich): *"I
always like to know what music I'm using before I start cutting."* S10: Blizzard chose a temp track at
the animatic stage. S24: Neubert manually beat-synced clip motion.

**And this is a finding about the product, not only about the craft.** The studio's phases run
`research → script → frames → score → cut`. Score sits *after* Frames, so the picture is planned
before the thing that is supposed to decide where its acts can fall. The registry states the tooling
failure exactly: "A system where beats carry timings and music is attached afterwards has encoded the
inverted dependency, and every structural check it runs will be measured against positions the music
does not mark." See `r3` in [`OPEN-QUESTIONS.md`](OPEN-QUESTIONS.md).

## 7. Derive downward, never upward

> "**Cut the longest rung first**, and derive downward. A ladder built upward from a spot produces a
> long cut that is a padded spot."

So this template is where a promotional family starts, and
[`teaser`](../../../teaser/steps/01-script/PATTERNS.md) is derived *from* it. That ordering is the one
structural relationship between the two templates, and it runs one way.

## 8. The spine is a default and a diagnostic, never a gate

The registry says this about its own doctrine, and it is the most important line in the subject for
anyone building a checker:

> "Award-winning cutters in that lane map a work's **mood** rather than its plot, on the explicit
> reasoning that the story is not what sells the ticket […] Both positions are held by practitioners
> with better track records than the doctrine has. So: **use the spine as the default and as the
> diagnostic, never as the gate.** A cut that deviates from it is either a specialty-lane choice or a
> defect, and **the structure alone cannot tell you which.**"

## 9. What this means for the Script step's UI

1. **Parts as first-class objects**, named — cold open, introduction, escalation rungs, reset, climax,
   button — not a flat beat list. The reset is a part, not a gap.
2. **A raised-variable field per rung**, from a closed set (scale · threat · speed · intimacy · cost).
   "A rung that declares no variable, or repeats the previous one, is mechanically detectable — which
   turns the most common structural defect in the form into something a checker can surface."
3. **A connector shown between adjacent rungs** — but / therefore / and-then — with *and then* marked
   as the defect.
4. **A payer field on every promise-bearing beat**, surfaced as incomplete when empty (§5) — and
   never as a block.
5. **The withholding budget as a project-level object**, five named assets × {spend, imply, hold},
   with a recorded sentence for every `spend`. It cannot live on a beat; it binds to the campaign.
6. **An energy curve the author can see**, so the dip is visible as a shape rather than inferred from
   a list.
7. **Nothing that reports a green structural verdict as "this works."** §8.

## 10. The machine-readable half — [`params.json`](params.json)

Opened 2026-08-29. Until then this template was the only one in the library with
`PATTERNS.md` and no `params.json`, and the reason was good:
[`OPEN-QUESTIONS.md`](OPEN-QUESTIONS.md) `r2` observed that about half the fields
are enumerations the doctrine states outright, and **every field that is a number
is missing its evidence** — so "a half-real `params.json` is read as wholly real."

What changed is not the evidence. It is that the rules were living in **two
places**: as prose here, and as TypeScript literals in
`app/_phases/script/trailer/structure.ts` — the checker that gates a creator's
work. Two copies with nothing holding them together is the drift this library
exists to prevent, and the copy the creator actually meets was the one with no
citations attached.

So the file exists, and `r2`'s objection is answered by shape rather than
ignored:

- **Every value carries its own label, its registry path and the quoted line.**
  A consumer cannot read the number without reading how strongly it is known.
  Every value in the file is `doctrine_only: true` and `n: 0`. **Nothing in it
  is MEASURED**, and the file says so where a reader cannot miss it.
- **Every hedged quantity stayed out**, in a `not_encoded` block that names the
  hedge, quotes it, and says what would settle it: the ~10s rung floor, the 3–5
  rung count (our arithmetic, not the source's claim), the n=130 shot-length
  curve, the "margin a viewer would notice", the withholding defaults (§ `r6`'s
  contested trade — a default would erase the registry's own hedge), and the
  per-rung part counts.
- **The checker consumes it.** `structure.ts` reads the legal beat kinds, the
  one-variable-per-rung rule, the reset counts and the advisory-rule list from
  the file and validates them at import; a params file naming a role or a beat
  kind the type union does not have fails loudly rather than silently checking
  something else. The verdicts did not move when the numbers did — that was the
  acceptance condition, and `npm run check:trailer-structure` is where it is
  held.
- **One value is INFERRED and is flagged as the only one:** the table of which
  beat kinds are legal in which movement. The doctrine says what each part is
  *for* and never enumerates it.

The energy curve's seven heights are **not** in the file and are not craft.
They are drawing coordinates, they say so at their use site
(`app/_phases/script/trailer/cut.ts` → `PRESENTATION`), the surface says so to
the user ("a shape read from the parts — not a measurement"), and `params.json`
records the refusal rather than hosting the numbers.

## 11. Confidence and limits

- **n=0.** No trailer has been torn down here.
- **The two MEASURED figures were measured on something else** — theatrical film trailers (n=130 over
  sixty years; a nine-decade centre of gravity with no stated n). Nobody has checked that they
  transfer to AI-composed cuts.
- **The spine is a default, not a law** (§8), and the specialty lane is a real, successful deviation.
- **Withholding is contested, deliberately.** "The most consistently reported finding from audience
  testing is that viewers say the trailer did not show them *enough*, which pushes campaigns toward
  revelation […] Nobody in the practitioner literature claims to have solved it." A tool that treats
  every revealing cut as incompetence "will mis-diagnose every campaign that made the trade
  knowingly."
- **No engagement data, and there cannot be a structural substitute for it.** "A structural checker
  can establish that a cut is malformed; it cannot establish that a cut works."
