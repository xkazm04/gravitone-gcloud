# Script patterns — ad-spot-30 (20–45s, target 30s)

Read **[`ad-social-15/steps/01-script/PATTERNS.md`](../../../ad-social-15/steps/01-script/PATTERNS.md)
first.** Its §1–§5 cover the brief, angle seeding, the screening gates, the honest risk and the timed
shot list. They hold for this template unchanged, because they are rules about the *idea* and the
*schema*, not about the length. Restating them here would put two copies in the library. This file
covers only what the 30s rung changes: §6 (parts and order) and §7 (the fields), as they differ.

**Sources:** n=0. Doctrine from the registry's `short-ad-structure`, `still-to-motion-direction` and
`ad-finishing`, quoted by technique.

---

## 1. Idea round: the same, with one added screen

Everything in the sibling's §1–§4 applies. One addition, from `length-rungs`:

> "**When an idea cannot be told in fifteen seconds without its turn**, it is a thirty-second idea."

The Idea prompt should say per idea whether it **needs** its turn. That is not a defect at this
length. It tells the creator that this idea has no 15s sibling, which matters if the brief wants a
cut-down later.

## 2. Parts at 30s: all six

OBSERVED · `length-rungs`. The Scenario prompt lays out the parts in this order:

| Part | What it does, quoted |
|---|---|
| **Hook** | "the first shot, tied to the product" |
| **Set-up** | "the situation or problem made legible" |
| **Truth** | "the product doing the thing that makes the proposition believable" |
| **Turn** | "the idea's leap developed: the extreme pushed further, the analogy extended, a second rung of the demonstration" |
| **Payoff** | "the question answered, because of the product" |
| **End card** | "brand, call to action, one line" |

With six shots at most, some parts share a shot. **Merge set-up into hook, or truth into payoff**,
which are the merges the 15s rung makes anyway. **Never merge the turn into the payoff at 30s.** That
is the 15s structure, and it leaves the spot with seconds and nothing to fill them.

## 3. The order: same as the sibling, slightly more patient

- **Hook first and product-tied** (`product-tied-hook`), but a wide or square spot often runs on a
  placement the viewer chose. The technique's own exception: "a slower opening that builds a situation
  can earn its seconds. The tie to the product still holds; the urgency does not." So the set-up may
  get a real shot, and the first shot still opens a question the product answers.
- **Brand: cue early, pulses, card** (`brand-presence-timing`). This matters more at 30s: there is
  room for three or four pulses. The rule is "each return is short; the brand is a pulse, not a
  watermark." An established brand may hold its first *full* appearance to "the middle third"; a new
  brand may not.
- **Payoff before ask; one ask; no button after** (`payoff-before-ask`). These are unchanged.

## 4. The arithmetic gets tight against the shot cap

OBSERVED · `timed-shot-list`, applied to a 30s runtime and a 6-shot cap:

1. **Reserve the end card first.** For a longer line, use its reading time (sibling §7).
2. The remaining ~26–27s goes over **at most six shots**, an average of ≥4.4s. **Every shot ≤ one
   clip's usable span** ("expect the usable part of a clip to be its first seconds"). The scenario
   should therefore prefer **fewer, longer-held shots that move by camera**, not six shots that each
   need a full generated clip.
3. **A continuous action longer than one clip** is split "at a real cut point — a change of angle or a
   reaction — chosen now, not where the generator's cap happens to fall". At 30s this happens, and it
   costs a shot from the cap of six.
4. **Allocate by function.** The turn and the payoff get the most time. The set-up gets the least that
   makes it legible.

## 5. Fields: what differs from the sibling's §7

- **`image`**: compose for **16:9 or 1:1 natively**. If the project might also want a 9:16 version,
  "compose the key image with the second aspect's crop in mind — the subject and the product inside the
  region both frames share" (`reframe-per-aspect`). A tall crop of a wide frame keeps under a third of
  its width.
- **`motion`**: the same line shape (one camera move, one action, a speed word, a medium phrase).
  Longer shots favour **camera-led motion with a still subject**: "Prefer camera motion over product
  motion" (`product-fidelity`). Shorter clips hold better: one vendor recommends "stitching two
  four-second clips over generating one eight-second clip" (`motion-amount-tradeoff`, vendor-grade).
- **`super`**: the same hold arithmetic. At 30s there is room for two or three supers. Each still
  restates or sharpens the one proposition: "The words are a second ad" when they extend it.
- **`musicMood`**: one bed whose **resolution lands on the end card**. "A card that arrives mid-phrase
  reads as an interruption."

## 6. Confidence and limits

Everything in the sibling's §8, plus:

- **The 30s rung's value over the 15s is unsettled**: one 2026 campaign, one brand (template, gap 2).
- **The shot-cap tension is this studio's, not the doctrine's.** The registry's arithmetic assumes
  shots can be added. Six is a pipeline decision, and `s1` asks whether it holds at this length.
