# Basic Logic — *Why Is Black America So Racist?*

**Catalogued for its skeleton, not its execution.** The Adjudication structure is genuinely useful and
is abstracted into [`ENGINES.md`](../../../../../ENGINES.md) § D. The content is not a model for the
honesty standard this library is trying to encode, and the second section says why in structural terms
that can be checked without holding any position on the subject.

## The skeleton — the part worth keeping

```
[0:00]  HOOK: a forced-choice quiz — A denounce / B stay silent / C give him half a million dollars
[0:20]  THE CLAIM: "consistently, the most racist group is black Americans"
[0:30]  EVIDENCE STACK
[1:05]  THE QUESTION: "But why are they like that?"
[1:15]  THE PROMISE: "In this video, I go over the main theories."

── CANDIDATE 1 · historical grievances ───────────────────────
[1:35]  STATE IT fairly: slavery, Jim Crow → today's anti-white racism
[1:50]  TEST: racism follows cause and effect; nobody alive was a slave or a slave owner
[2:10]  TEST: grievances usually fade — the Irish, Asians, Italians "moved on"
[2:25]  VERDICT: "mostly rationalization, not the root cause"
   THEREFORE
── CANDIDATE 2 · they are taught to hate ─────────────────────
[2:35]  "This one is more compelling." — education and media
[3:00]  Evidence: coverage of police shootings
[3:20]  The challenge (see below)
   THEREFORE
── CANDIDATE 3 · they are paid to hate ───────────────────────
[3:45]  "being racist against white people is profitable" — donations, DEI, reparations, grifters
[4:25]  MECHANISM: a feedback loop with candidate 2
[4:45]  VERDICT + close
```

**What genuinely transfers** is the per-candidate shape: each theory is *named*, *stated in a form its
believers would recognise*, *tested against a stated principle*, and *given an explicit verdict* — and
the verdicts differ ("mostly rationalization" vs "more compelling"), which is what makes it read as
weighing rather than listing. The numbered enumeration doubles as navigation, solving the mid-form
promise problem cheaply. That four-part shape is Engine D and is worth stealing wholesale.

## On the execution — why this is not the honesty exemplar

This video was supplied as an example of "research, logic, honesty". Four structural properties say
otherwise:

1. **The premise is never a candidate.** All three theories presuppose the measured claim and ask only
   *why*. An honest adjudication puts "the thing may be mismeasured, or may not be real" in the
   candidate set. Here the load-bearing claim is settled inside 30 seconds and never revisited — so
   the viewer is walked past it while feeling rigorously informed.
2. **The central empirical claim is unsourced.** "Study after study" with no study named, supporting
   the sentence the entire video rests on.
3. **The counter-evidence is pre-excluded.** *"List a systemic disadvantage that black people have,
   but you can't just say in effect like black people go to prison more. You have to mention a current
   day unique legal discrimination"* — a challenge whose most common answers are ruled inadmissible by
   its own phrasing, which makes it unfalsifiable by construction. Compare PolyMatter at 5:40, which
   states the opposing case at full strength *before* answering it.
4. **Individual acts are attributed to a group.** One stabbing opens the video as a group-identity
   quiz; riots, monuments and a cult are then listed as "their exploits".

**Corroborated by measurement.** Causal-opener density is **15%** — the lowest of ten sources, against
PolyMatter's 38% on a comparably contested subject. That number measures how much a script *derives*
versus *asserts*, and this is what a low score looks like.

**The product risk, stated plainly:** a generator that takes this as its honesty reference learns to
produce polemic wearing the costume of analysis. Adjudication is the engine where that failure is
hardest to catch, because the *appearance* of weighing alternatives persuades whether or not any
weighing occurred. Take the skeleton from here; take the standard from PolyMatter and Economics
Explained, which argue contested subjects while sourcing their numbers and steel-manning the other
side.
