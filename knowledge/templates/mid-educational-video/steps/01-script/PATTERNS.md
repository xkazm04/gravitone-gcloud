# Script patterns — mid-length educational video (3–6 min)

Read [`CRAFT-BASELINE.md`](../../../../CRAFT-BASELINE.md) and [`ENGINES.md`](../../../../ENGINES.md)
first; this document only covers what is specific to **3–6 minutes**.

**Sources:** n=3, each read in full — Fireship *DeepSeek* (4:40, Briefing) · Basic Logic *Three
Theories* (5:01, Adjudication) · PolyMatter *Apple's Money Problem* (8:04, Reversal Chain).

---

## 1. The shape: 2–3 movements, announced

MEASURED · all three declare their structure early, but **two different ways**:

**As questions** (the form inherited from short and long):
> Fireship, 2:48 — *"but what does that even mean?"* · 3:48 — *"when to use a Chain of Thought model
> instead of a regular large language model?"*

**As topics** — new at this length, and the more elegant form:
> PolyMatter, 0:23 — *"The strange story about Apple's finances explains **how it got so wealthy**,
> **why this is a problem**, and **what they'll buy next**."*

Three noun-phrases, delivered in one breath at 23 seconds, and the video executes them in exactly
that order. It does the same work as Economics Explained's three questions with half the words, and
it scans better in a title-shaped subject.

**Basic Logic** uses the third form, native to Adjudication: **numbered candidates.** *"Theory one,
historical grievances… Theory two, they're taught to hate… Theory three, they're paid to hate."*
The enumeration *is* the navigation.

INFERRED · pick the promise form from the engine — questions for Reversal Chain and Briefing, topics
when the movements are naturally nouns, numbered candidates for Adjudication.

## 2. Turn budget: 3–5

MEASURED · PolyMatter's Reversal Chain across 8 minutes:

| Time | Turn |
|---|---|
| 1:00 | *"there's an exception to every rule. And that exception is always America."* |
| 2:20 | *"But what about the 30% of revenue Apple makes in the US?"* |
| 3:40 | *"But because Apple makes so much more money, it's in an unusual position… it's all frozen just out of reach."* |
| 3:55 | *"And the problem is actually much deeper than that."* ⟨escalation⟩ |
| 4:10 | *"To you and I this would be the opposite of a problem… And maybe it should pay higher taxes."* ⟨self-attack⟩ |
| 5:40 | *"implied in this argument is another…"* ⟨the hidden premise — see §4⟩ |

Roughly **one turn per 60–90 seconds** of essay. Below three turns at this length the script reads as
a lecture; above five it stops letting any conclusion stand long enough to matter.

Compare the scaling across the corpus: **2 turns in 64s · 3–5 at 3–6 min · 4+ at 18 min.** Turn
*density* falls as length rises — long form buys room for evidence between turns, not more turns.

## 3. The digression budget

MEASURED · Fireship spends **~35 of 280 seconds (12%)** on material that advances no beat: Sam Altman
at the inauguration, a meme template, Zuckerberg, a security researcher getting ChatGPT to DoS
websites. It is pure persona maintenance.

INFERRED · **mid-length is the first format that can afford a digression, and ~10% is the ceiling.**
Two conditions in the observed case: it sits *after* the promise is made (0:45) and *before* the
mechanism starts (2:40), and it stays inside the subject's world. A digression before the promise
would spend the hook; one during the mechanism would break the chain.

Neither PolyMatter nor Basic Logic digresses at all. This is an **engine-dependent allowance**:
Briefing tolerates it because the viewer is being oriented and the presenter's judgment is part of
the product. Adjudication cannot afford it — a theory left hanging while the host does a bit is a
theory the viewer stops tracking.

## 4. Devices that appear at this length

### Surfacing the hidden premise (PolyMatter, 5:40) — the strongest move in the corpus
Rather than arguing against a conclusion, name the **unstated assumption** underneath it:

> *"Articles like this one suggest Apple buy Tesla, Activision, and Netflix. And to an investor, this
> makes perfect sense… **But implied in this argument is another.** If Microsoft and Google
> successfully acquire companies left and right, but Apple rarely does, they must be sleeping on the
> job."*

Then it attacks the *premise* — Microsoft paid 8× more for LinkedIn than Apple has ever paid for
anything, and nobody concluded Microsoft was bad with money. INFERRED · this needs mid-length to work:
you must state the opposing case fully and fairly first, which costs 20–30 seconds, and it is the most
respectful form of disagreement available.

### Institutions given dialogue (PolyMatter, throughout)
*"Ireland rolled its eyes and shook its head"* · *"This is where Apple, Google, almost every major
company says, 'Hmm… Actually, I think we're gonna keep our money out here for now.'"* · *"it can wink
at the IRS."*

Abstract actors become characters with motives. It is the cheapest available fix for the driest
possible subject, and it belongs to the **reference world** in [`TONE.md`](../../../../TONE.md) —
PolyMatter's signature, not a universal rule.

### The frame that gives the viewer somewhere to stand (Fireship, 0:10)
> *"there's two types of people in the tech world right now… the pessimists… and the optimists…
> **pessimists sound smart while optimists make money**"*

A Briefing's viewer has no position yet, so the script hands them the axis before the evidence.
OBSERVED · the aphorism is the thesis in portable form — the same job as a reframe close, done at the
front.

### The skeptical check (Fireship, 1:58)
> *"let me remind you once again that you should never trust benchmarks… this company Epoch AI, which
> provides a popular math benchmark, only recently disclosed that they've been funded by OpenAI, which
> feels a bit like a conflict of interest"*

A move against the script's own enthusiasm, with a concrete instance. **A Briefing without one is a
press release.** Same family as Engine A's self-attack and as EE's epistemic disclaimer.

### Declared exposure (Fireship, 0:04)
*"which I'm stupidly paying $200 a month for right now."* The presenter's stake, stated in the first
five seconds, before any judgment is offered.

## 5. Sponsor placement

MEASURED, three data points, one rule:

| | Sponsor | Placed |
|---|---|---|
| Fireship | 35s of 280 (12%) | 4:00 — after the mechanism, before the close |
| PolyMatter | ~2min of 484 (25%) | 6:52 — after the reframe, effectively post-roll |
| Economics Explained | 60s of 1073 | 2:20 — after the hook and promise land |

**All three sit after a payoff and never inside an argument.** INFERRED · the slot is *"the viewer has
just been given something"* — the promise (EE), the mechanism (Fireship), or the thesis (PolyMatter).
The studio's template should model a sponsor slot explicitly with those three legal positions, and
**budget essay time separately from runtime** — a 6-minute video may be a 4:30 essay.

## 6. Rate is not a target at this length

MEASURED · 156 (Basic Logic) · 176 (PolyMatter) · 234 (Fireship). A 78-wpm spread on the same format.

The low end is not slower *speech* so much as **more room given to visuals** — PolyMatter is densely
animated and its words leave space for the picture to land. The high end is talking-head-plus-code
with no such obligation.

INFERRED · **rate is a consequence of the visual plan, which the Script step does not yet know.**
Treat it as a range at this stage, resolve it in the Frames step, and never let a "pace" dial move the
beat chain. See [`TONE.md`](../../../../TONE.md) §2.

## 7. What this means for the Script step's UI

Additive to the short-form UI contract:

1. **A movement rail** — 2–3 movements, each with a name, a promise form (question / topic / numbered
   candidate) and a second budget.
2. **A turn counter with a band** — 3–5 for this length; flag below 3 (lecture) and above 5 (churn).
3. **A sponsor slot** with the three legal positions, excluded from the essay word budget.
4. **A digression allowance** — visible, capped ~10%, and *disabled for Adjudication*.
5. **Currency metadata** — "how long is this true for". A tech Briefing dated by construction should
   say so, and the Library should be able to find scripts whose facts have expired.
6. **An honesty checklist for Adjudication** — the three D-rigged tells from `ENGINES.md`, surfaced
   as review items, not as blockers: is the premise itself a candidate; can any candidate win; is the
   counter-evidence admitted at full strength.

## 8. Confidence and limits

- **n=3, one per engine, and one source (PolyMatter) is 34% over the band.**
- **Two of three have no punctuated captions** — Fireship's ASR gives no sentence-level data at all.
- **The 10% digression ceiling is one observation** on one channel whose persona is the product.
- **The sponsor rule rests on three placements** and is the kind of thing that varies by sponsor
  contract rather than by craft.
- **Basic Logic is catalogued for its skeleton, not its execution** — see `ENGINES.md` § D-honest vs
  D-rigged. Its 15% causal density is the lowest measured and is consistent with a script that
  asserts more than it derives; do not use it as a rate or density reference.
