# Open questions — mid-length script step

---

### m1 · Does the Adjudication honesty checklist actually catch a rigged script? ⭐
`ENGINES.md` § D-rigged gives three structural tells, derived from one negative example. They are
untested against (a) an honest adjudication, to check they don't produce false positives, and (b) a
*subtle* rigged one, where the premise is smuggled rather than asserted.
*Settles it:* two more Adjudication sources — one from a source with a strong sourcing reputation
(Vox, FT, The Economist's video desk), one from a channel with a known lean. Compare their causal
densities and their treatment of the premise.
**This matters more than any other question in this template**, because economy/politics is the
studio's stated subject area and Adjudication is its natural engine.

### m2 · Is the ~10% digression ceiling real, or one channel's habit?
One observation (Fireship, 12%), on a channel whose persona *is* the product. Two of three sources
digress not at all.
*Settles it:* three more Briefing-engine sources across different channels.

### m3 · Where exactly does the sponsor go, and does it cost retention?
Three placements, all "after a payoff". The rule is plausible but rests on very little, and sponsor
position is often contractual rather than editorial.
*Settles it:* timestamps across 6–8 sponsored mid-length videos — cheap, mechanical, no reading
required.

### m4 · How does rate resolve against visual density?
156 → 234 wpm on one format, and the spread is clearly about how much the picture carries. The Script
step currently emits a range and defers. That deferral is only acceptable while the Frames step is
unwritten.
*Settles it:* a shot-change count against narration for the same three sources. Properly the first
question of the **02-frames** step.

### m5 · Do the two-question and three-topic promise forms behave differently?
Both observed once each. Whether "topics" scale to three movements as reliably as questions, and
whether a viewer navigates as well without an audible question mark, is unknown.
*Settles it:* two more sources using the topic form.

### m6 · What is the currency half-life by subject?
`PATTERNS.md` §7 requires currency metadata but has no data on what values are realistic. A tech
Briefing may be stale in weeks; a tax-mechanism explainer stayed useful for years apart from one beat.
*Settles it:* revisit the three sources and mark which claims have expired since publication —
possible right now, since PolyMatter's is eight years old.
