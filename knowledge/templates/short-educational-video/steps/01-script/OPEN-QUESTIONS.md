# Open questions — script step

What we do **not** know, and the source that would settle it. Ordered by how much they block the
step's design. An answered question moves into `PATTERNS.md` with its evidence label and is struck
through here, naming the run that answered it.

---

### q1 · What other narrative engines exist? ⭐ still the top blocker
Now **three** engines, still one witness each — Reversal Chain, Effort/Payoff Gap, and (new, run 2)
Parallel Case. The engine picker is the step's first decision, so a missing engine is a whole class of
video the tool cannot compose. Run 2 found a new engine from two videos, which suggests the taxonomy
is nowhere near closed.
Still to look for: a **detective** engine (evidence accumulating toward a reveal — Johnny Harris,
Wendover), a **two rivals** engine (comparison as structure), a **personal stake** engine.
*Settles it:* 4–6 videos across visibly different channels, mapped as beat chains, looking for repeated
shapes. **Deliberately pick channels unlike the four studied** — all four so far are single-narrator
explainers, two of them from the same lineage.

### q3 · How does a writer get from a topic to a tension? ⭐ blocking step 1 of the procedure
Unchanged and still the weakest link. The composition procedure starts "find the tension" — the one
step we cannot describe. All four sources *have* one; none shows how it was found.
Run 2 added one clue: MinutePhysics manufactures tension from a **question whose intuitive answer is
wrong** ("does pressure keep it from freezing?" → "No"), and MinuteEarth from a **pattern that looks
like a coincidence** (biggest land animals AND biggest sea animals both eat tiny things). Both are
*findable moves*, which is encouraging — there may be a small catalogue of tension-finding operations.
*Settles it:* teardowns of 5+ videos on well-known subjects where the tension had to be manufactured,
catalogued by which move was used. Until then the step treats tension-finding as a **user decision
with prompts**, not an automated one.

### q4 · What does a real production document look like?
Unchanged. Everything is reverse-engineered from finished output, which shows what was decided but
never what was rejected or in what order.
*Settles it:* published scripts/outlines, a "how we write our videos" video, or a writers'-room
interview. Kurzgesagt have discussed a dozen-drafts process publicly, so material likely exists.

### q6 · How does the beat structure drive the visuals?
Unchanged, and about to become urgent — it is properly the **first question of the 02-frames step**.
*Settles it:* a shot-change analysis against the transcript; needs the video, not just captions.

### q7 · Is "a joke may not occupy a beat" a real rule or two presenters' habit?
Partly addressed by run 2 and now sharper. **Neither short source is funny** — MinutePhysics and
MinuteEarth are warm but not comic, and both work. So humor is clearly **not required** by the format.
The remaining question is narrower: when humor *is* present, must it carry information (as it does in
Fireship and EE)?
*Settles it:* a source with humor that is *decorative* rather than load-bearing, to see whether it
costs anything.

### q8 · Does the engine survive translation to a mundane subject?
Partly addressed. MinuteEarth's subject (why big animals eat small food) is genuinely ordinary, and
Engine C carries it — evidence that a strong engine can rescue a subject with no inherent drama. But
all four sources remain *inherently interesting*.
*Settles it:* an exemplar on a deliberately boring subject (how a mortgage works, why bread rises).

### q9 · Where does the act count break in the 3–10 minute middle? — NEW
MEASURED at both ends now: **1 question** at ≤3 min, **3 questions** at 17:53. The whole middle band is
untested, and it is where a lot of real content lives.
*Settles it:* two sources at ~5 min and ~8 min.

### q10 · Does Engine C work at long form? — NEW
Parallel Case has exactly one witness at exactly one length (149s). Whether "familiar domain →
unfamiliar domain" can carry 15 minutes, or whether it needs multiple transfers to do so, is unknown.
*Settles it:* a long-form video with a domain-transfer structure.

### q11 · Is the sponsor slot structural? — NEW
MEASURED · MinutePhysics spends **35% of a 100-second video** on sponsor read; EE places its at
2:20–3:20, after the hook has fully landed. Both put it *after* the promise and *before* the payoff.
If that placement is a rule, the studio's template should model a sponsor slot rather than pretend it
doesn't exist.
*Settles it:* sponsor placement across 6–8 sponsored videos — cheap to measure, just needs timestamps.

## Answered

- ~~**q2 · Can a 2-minute script hold a true reversal?**~~ **YES — answered by run 2 (2026-08-11).**
  MinutePhysics runs **two full reversals in a 64-second essay body**, ~18s each, against an inferred
  floor of 60–90s *per reversal*. The inference was wrong by 3–4×. The anatomy does not need the time;
  it sheds two specific steps (the concrete analogy and the generalisation). Now `PATTERNS.md` §5.
  **Method lesson: do not scale timings from long sources — go measure a short one.**
- ~~**q5 · Does the question stack scale to two questions?**~~ **No — it scales to ONE.** Answered by
  run 2: both short sources ask exactly one question, in the first 12 seconds. Now `PATTERNS.md` §3.

## Retired

- **Delivery-mechanics questions** (wpm ceilings, sentence-length distributions). Run 2 extended the

  subject, not inputs. Re-open only if a produced script fails for a *delivery* reason rather than a
  *composition* one.
