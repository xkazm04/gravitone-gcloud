## What makes it work

**The disproportion is the lesson.** The video spends a third of its runtime on excruciating manual
memory manipulation and pays off with two words of output. That gap is simultaneously the joke and the
pedagogy: you cannot be told what this language costs, you have to be walked through it. A "clearer"
version that skipped the labour would teach less.

Consequences that generalise:

1. **The longest beat is the demonstration, not the explanation.** The mechanism section (0:32–0:50,
   18s) is *shorter* than the example that uses it (42s). The natural instinct — explain thoroughly,
   demo briefly — is inverted.
2. **The joke is load-bearing.** "Delete your operating system" is both the gag and the setup
   instruction; removing it removes information. Same for "like cavemen", which is the thesis restated
   as a punchline.
3. **Absence is reframed as character.** "No variables, functions or classes" could be a limitation;
   the punch beat turns it into the point of the thing.
4. **Zero hedging.** MEASURED · 0.0 per 1k. The subject is fully knowable, so every sentence is
   declarative — the exact inverse of Engine A, and for a good reason.
5. **The inclusive "we" is procedural**, not editorial: *"we'll need to set each number"*, *"we can
   move the pointer"*. 23.3 per 1k, the highest pronoun density in either source. It puts the viewer's
   hands on the keyboard.
6. **The title is a promise the script keeps out loud** before the CTA.
