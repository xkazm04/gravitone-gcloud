## Engine C — the Parallel Case

> Establish a rule in a **familiar** domain, with its full mechanism → transfer it to an **unfamiliar**
> domain → the rule holds **but needs a twist** → the twist deepens the rule rather than breaking it →
> reframe.

MEASURED · the split is almost exactly half and half: land case **0:15–1:22** (67s), ocean case
**1:23–2:10** (47s), close 2:10–2:16.

Why it is a distinct engine and not a Reversal Chain: **the viewer is never told they were wrong.**
Engine A's pleasure is being corrected; this engine's pleasure is *pattern recognition* — watching a
rule you just learned survive a harder test. The single "but" in the ocean half (phytoplankton aren't
enough) is not a reversal of the thesis, it is an obstacle the thesis overcomes.

**Use when** the idea is a principle with a surprising instance — where the interesting thing is not
"you're wrong" but "this holds even here, for a reason you wouldn't guess."

INFERRED · the familiar half must come first and must be fully mechanised. The transfer only works
because the viewer already owns the rule; the ocean half can then be 30% shorter and still land.

## Devices worth stealing

### The reverse analogy, at the close
Standard analogy explains the unfamiliar with the familiar. This runs **backwards**: having explained
krill swarms, it takes the new mechanism *back* to the established domain as a thought experiment —
grasshopper swarms for elephants — and notes the elephants would then get even bigger.

It confirms the rule rather than explaining it, and it does so by making a prediction. INFERRED ·
this is a **closing** device; it works because the viewer has both domains in mind at once, which is
only true at the end.

### The pre-empted objection, inline
*"And even if there were, the giants' massive bodies aren't mobile or agile enough to actually
capture that food."* Same family as Economics Explained's "this is not some special rule invented to
be mean to North Korea", compressed to a clause. OBSERVED in three of four sources — likely a general
rule.

### Branding placed after the gap
*"Hi, I'm David and this is MinuteEarth"* at **0:12** — after the question is asked, never before.
This **refines** the anti-pattern recorded from the first two sources: the rule is not "no
housekeeping", it is **"nothing before the hook."** Three seconds of identity is affordable once the
viewer wants something.

### A pun as the reframe
*"food things come in small packages"* — riding on "good things come in small packages". Same
structural job as EE's "borrowed prosperity from the past": one repeatable sentence that re-describes
the whole video.
