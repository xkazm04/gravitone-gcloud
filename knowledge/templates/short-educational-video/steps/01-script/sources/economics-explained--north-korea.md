## What makes it work

1. **The answer is given at 1:00 of a 17:53 video.** Nothing is withheld. The tension is never *what
   happened* — it is *how can that possibly be true*, and that question survives being answered
   because each act reveals the previous answer was too simple.
2. **Four reversals, each more interesting than what it replaced.** The viewer is never allowed to
   settle. Critically, reversal 2 attacks the script's *own* reversal 1 — that self-correction is the
   difference between thinking and arguing.
3. **The question stack is stated out loud and answered in order**, with an explicit announcement
   entering act 3. The viewer always knows where they are.
4. **Three analogies in eighteen minutes**, each spent on a step a smart viewer would otherwise refuse
   (barter, inventory accounting, "this rule isn't anti-NK"), each physical, each dropped immediately.
5. **Every number is converted to a felt scale.** Not one significant figure is left as a bare number.
6. **The close re-describes rather than summarises** — "borrowed prosperity from the past" is the
   sentence a viewer repeats to someone else, and it recasts the entire video.
