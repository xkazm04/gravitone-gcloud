## What this changes

### 1. The reversal anatomy compresses — and the cuts are specific

Checked against the six-step anatomy derived from Economics Explained (`PATTERNS.md` §5):

| Step | Long form (EE) | Here | |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1. Let the viewer settle | ✓ ~40s | ✓ **11s** — "pressure CAN melt ice", with two data points | **survives** |
| 2. Signal the turn | ✓ a full sentence of warning | ✓ **four words** — "But that's for fresh water." | **survives, compressed** |
| 3. Mechanism, not verdict | ✓ | ✓ salinity shifts the freezing point, quantified | **survives** |
| 4. Concrete analogy | ✓ the fridge factory | ✗ **none in the entire video** | **cut** |
| 5. Generalise so it isn't a trick | ✓ the US petroleum reserve | ✗ | **cut** |
| 6. Escalate once | ✓ | ✓ reversal 2 | **survives** |

**INFERRED — steps 4 and 5 are the expensive ones, and short form spends its seconds on 1–3–6
instead.** That is a much more useful rule than a duration floor: it says *what to drop*, not *don't
try*.

### 2. "Answer early" is more extreme than the long-form source suggested

Economics Explained gives its thesis at 1:00 of 17:53. This gives its answer as **the second word of
the video**. Then, crucially:

> *"But you were probably hoping for a more in-depth answer"*

That line is the whole technique. It states out loud that **answering the question did not close the
gap** — the gap was never *what*, it was *why*. One sentence buys the remaining sixty seconds.

### 3. The number ladder replaces the analogy

With no analogy anywhere, the concretising device is a **repeated measurement across conditions**:
ice melts at −4°C at 500×, −9°C at 1000×; then the same ladder again for salt water: −2°C surface,
−6°C at 500×, −11°C at the trench. The viewer learns the *shape* of the relationship by seeing the
same number move.

MEASURED · 28.6 numeric tokens per 1k words — the highest of any source studied.

### 4. Hedging spikes when the mechanism is genuinely messy

MEASURED · **18.2 hedges per 1k** — nearly triple Economics Explained's 6.5, and the highest measured.
Concentrated in one place: *"for various reasons related to circulation and salinity and temperature
and convection and the fact salt water freezes into freshwater ice… for complicated reasons"*.

OBSERVED · the script **declines to explain** the messy part and says so. It does not fake a mechanism
it hasn't got room for. That refusal is what lets a 64-second script touch a complicated subject
without lying — and it is the short-form version of EE's epistemic disclaimer.
