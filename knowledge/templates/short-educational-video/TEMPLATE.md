# Template — short educational video (1–3 min)

The first project template the studio supports end to end. One idea, explained well, in the length a
person will actually watch.

## What this format actually is

**A question chain with facts hung on it.** Not facts arranged on a timeline — that distinction is the
whole template, and getting it wrong is the failure mode the knowledge library exists to prevent
(see [`steps/01-script/PATTERNS.md`](steps/01-script/PATTERNS.md) §0).

The viewer stays because each beat makes them want the next one. Every other property of the format —
length, delivery, visual density — is downstream of that.

| Property | Value | Source |
|---|---|---|
| Narrative engine | **Reversal Chain**, **Effort/Payoff Gap** or **Parallel Case** — chosen first, before anything is written | OBSERVED · one exemplar each |
| Act structure | **1 question** asked aloud in the first 12s (short form); 3 at long form | MEASURED · n=4 |
| Beat linking | every adjacent pair joined by BUT or THEREFORE, never AND THEN | the one law · CRAFT-BASELINE §1 |
| Target duration | 60–180s of **essay** (net of sponsor) | MEASURED · studied shorts: 64s, 128s, 149s |
| Delivery rate | 197–252 wpm | MEASURED · n=4 |
| Voice | one narrator | OBSERVED · all four |
| Scope | exactly one thesis | OBSERVED · all four |

**Duration is a budget applied last**, not a design input. A 130-second video at ~215 wpm is roughly
425–525 words — about one page — and what fits is decided by how many *turns* the argument needs, not
by word count. MEASURED · a short-form reversal runs in **~18–25 seconds**, so a 2-minute script
comfortably holds two; it achieves that by dropping the analogy and the generalisation, not by
hurrying. Budget **essay time, not runtime** — one studied 100-second video was 35% sponsor.

## The steps

| # | Step | Studio phase | Knowledge |
|---|---|---|---|
| 01 | **Script** — idea → beat chain → spoken script + scene plan | Script | [`steps/01-script/`](steps/01-script/) — n=4, 3 engines |
| 02 | Frames — beats → key stills | Frames | not started |
| 03 | Motion — stills → moving shots | Motion | not started |
| 04 | Score — cut → music and sound | Score | not started |
| 05 | Cut — assemble, time, deliver | Cut | not started |

Steps 02–05 have no knowledge yet and the prototype should say so rather than guess. Deliberate
sequencing: **the content script is nailed first**, because every downstream step is scheduled against
the beats. A frame plan built on a script with no narrative engine inherits the defect and adds
production cost to it.

## What makes this template hard

1. **Density is not the product; causality is.** The failure mode is not "too long", it is "each
   sentence gives me no reason to hear the next one".
2. **The tension must be found before the script is written.** An idea with no counterintuitive,
   contested or absurd angle is a topic, not a video — and finding that angle is the step we can least
   describe today (OPEN-QUESTIONS q3).
3. **The engines are genuinely different products.** Same length, same subject-matter class, opposite
   construction — so engine choice cannot be a styling option applied afterwards. It also drives things
   a writer would assume were free choices: one studied script contains **zero numbers**, another
   **28.6 per 1,000 words**, and both are correct for their subject.
