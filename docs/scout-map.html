The redesign (spark scout-vision, 2026-10-25)
  1. The ladder. Spread genres while no project exists; prove a kept idea can be executed (storyboard → trial clips → piece); a project (channel, album, game) is born on the Board only after that.
  2. S1 shipped: prove before you spread. While 20+ kept scene/style ideas have no storyboard, a seed batch takes children plus at most 20 fresh (47 waiting at the cut). Storyboards: default 12, max 20, each applying a researched technique a keep files into PATTERNS.md. Music seeds carry instruments, tempo, mood, vocals; the Suno prompt is written from them.
  3. S2 shipped: genres and ventures. A genre is born when you keep a proposal resting on a kept storyboard; up to 2 trials a night per live genre (every panel clipped), 4 kept trials make a piece (best trial, rejected clips re-made, cut with LTX audio); a kept piece makes it publishable and opens a channel proposal. Ventures: channel, album (game in S4). Library kinds + a /scout venture filter. Next: S3 muse merged into scout as one character creator (named fictional characters in words, never real people, never source pixels); S4 POF asset-needs.json; S5 stats reel real data.
What the first map showed (before the redesign)
  1. Two roots, not one. Ideas, tracks and music grow from seed; styles, scene clips and findings grow from web source and have no seed parent (the app files them under "before seeds"). The skill numbers them as one night: 1a seeds, 1d ideas, then 2 discover. They are two chains that never meet except through the taste brief.
  2. Only one chain reaches a published video today: kept seed → track → visualizer (paused until Suno's limit resets, ~2026-11-06). Storyboard → full video and stats reel → real data are "later work" in the code; scene clips, styles, findings and directions end at a keep.
  3. Three different products hide under one word. A scout asset serves a game project; a muse scene is a still for a companion / roleplay app; only the rest claim to serve a YouTube video. The skill's first rule ("the product is a YouTube video") is true of the scout wing's seeds and ideas only.
  4. "Scene" means two things. Scout scene = a replica still plus a local LTX clip from a found keyframe. Muse scene = a 16:9 still of a kept character in a situation. Different inputs, different outputs, same word.
  5. Skill step order is not dependency order. Steps 1b-1e say "brief EARLY, before any search", yet ideas need a kept seed, storyboards need a kept idea from a previous night, and 0b "develop kept ideas first" sits inside step 4. Directions (4b) come after music (4c). See the two lists at the bottom.
  6. The missing top node. Nothing in either skill states which channel, series or video a night is working toward. Each node's popup ends with the question a keep of it should answer; that question is where a vision statement would plug in.
