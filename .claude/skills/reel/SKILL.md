# Reel: one table in, two cuts out, every number issued

`/reel` makes a **stat reel**: a data video with no narrator, where the motion of the data is the whole
show. This session is the only author, and what it writes is **code**. Nothing in a reel is generated by an
image or video model, and nothing calls a model while it renders. The split of work:

| Who | Does |
|---|---|
| You (the session) | choose the idea, find and assemble the table, check every dated event against a public page, write the page |
| `pipeline/reel.mts` | validate the table, measure it, compute the clock, issue the numbers into the page, lint, photograph, render, enforce the gates |
| The operator | decides at three stops: is this table worth a page, does the proof look right, is the cut good enough to publish |

Design notes: `docs/reel.md`. The page contract: `pipeline/strips/CONTRACT.md`, `pipeline/strips/REEL.md`
and `pipeline/reel/CUT.md` (it wins where they differ). The standard this follows is the registry's
`data-video-virality` and `data-video-art-direction` subjects (media-generation bundle); read the golden
path of each once per session before choosing an idea or writing a page.

## Resume first, always

```bash
npx tsx pipeline/reel.mts status <folder>
```

It prints the stage and the one verb to run next. A reel lives in one folder, by convention
`foundry-out/reels/<slug>/`; every verb is safe to run twice.

| Stage | What exists | Next |
|---|---|---|
| (none) | - | `new` |
| `idea` | `reel.json` | assemble the table, then `data` - **STOP 1** after it |
| `data` | `table.json`, `continuity.json` | `plan` |
| `planned` | `plan-short.json`, `plan-long.json` | `brief <cut>` |
| cut `briefed` | `<cut>/inputs/` | write `<cut>/out/strip.html`, then `proof` - **STOP 2** after it |
| cut `proofed` | `<cut>/proof/f-*.png` | `cut` - **STOP 3** after it |
| cut `cut` | `<cut>/render/strip.mp4`, `sheet.png`, `gates.json` | the other cut, or done |

**A stop is a stop.** Say what was made, show where to look, and end the turn. Do not run the next verb
because the previous one passed.

**Each stop is also a Board packet** the operator can decide on in the app: `pkt:reel:<slug>:data`,
`pkt:reel:<slug>:<cut>:proof`, `pkt:reel:<slug>:<cut>:cut`. The verdict is never yours: you do not keep, reject
or commit a reel packet, and you never write `verdicts.json`. `status` prints the verdicts recorded so far;
`cut` refuses a cut whose proof the operator rejected. Keeping a cut packet puts the cut on the Calendar as a
youtube slot (at the time in the keep's `calendar: youtube <ISO>` note, or the next day at 08:00 UTC when
there is none), so say plainly at STOP 3 when a cut is silent or its table is proof-only: the keep is what
schedules it.

## 1. The idea - `new`

```bash
npx tsx pipeline/reel.mts new <folder> --idea idea.json     # {title, hook, path}
```

- `hook` is a question, at most 90 characters. It never names who ends on top.
- `path` says why someone keeps watching, with names and dates: the lead changes, the collapse, the
  comeback. A payoff that lives only in the last frame is not a path.
- Take ideas from `knowledge/reel/backlog.md` (`/reel ideas` prints it) or bring one. Refuse the shapes that
  cannot hold a viewer whatever the topic: one record holder at a time, a list of yearly winners, a few
  decades of yearly points, a fill-in where things only switch on.

## 2. The table - `data` - STOP 1

The table is the controlled content. Assemble it as JSON (`lib/reel/types.ts` `ReelTable`):

```json
{ "v": 1, "unit": "wins", "granularity": "race", "cumulative": true,
  "periods": ["1950-05-13", "..."],
  "entities": [{ "id": "fangio", "name": "Juan Fangio", "values": [null, 1, 1, 2] }],
  "events": [{ "id": "e1", "period": 5, "title": "...", "date": "1950", "fact": "...", "url": "https://..." }],
  "source": { "name": "...", "url": "https://...", "licence": "...", "fetchedAt": "2026-11-17" } }
```

Rules that are yours, because the CLI cannot see them:

- **One period per real observation.** Never interpolate rows into the table to reach a step count.
- **The licence is read, not remembered.** Open the source's terms and copy what they say. A source that is
  non-commercial, or whose terms you could not read, may be used for a proof and **never for a published
  cut**: say so at the stop.
- **Every event is checked against a public page** whose url goes on the event. An event you cannot confirm
  is dropped, never guessed. 5 to 15 events; each is something the data visibly reacts to.
- `null` means no observation (the entity is drawn as absent); `0` means a real zero.
- Write the script that builds the table and keep it beside the reel (`<folder>/build-table.mjs`), so the
  table can be rebuilt and extended when the data moves.

```bash
npx tsx pipeline/reel.mts data <folder> --table table.json
```

It measures the table against the continuity gate and **refuses under a floor**, naming each: about 60 real
steps (yearly data: about 100 years and at least 10 entities), 5 entities on screen, 2 lead changes, 15
overtakes inside the top 10, 5 to 15 events. When it refuses, reframe before anything else: finer steps, a
longer span, more entities, the race among the chasers when the leader never changes. `--thin "<reason>"`
takes a table forward under a floor, and the reason is **the operator's words**, never yours.

**STOP 1.** Report the measured numbers (steps, lead changes with the leaders and their dates, overtakes),
the source and its licence as read, and whether this table may be published. The operator decides whether
it earns a page.

## 3. The bed - `track` (optional, before `plan`)

```bash
npx tsx pipeline/reel.mts track <folder> short --file bed.mp3
```

A reel's sound is one instrumental bed, played whole: the cut is planned to the track's real length, so a
track must sit inside the cut's range (30-60 s, 120-240 s) or it is refused. The ten lofi directions and the
Suno call for them are in `knowledge/reel/sound-prompts.md`. **Making a track spends the operator's Suno
credits: say the spend and get a yes first, every batch.** Attaching a different track restarts that cut,
because its clock changed. With no track the cut is silent and as long as its table asks.

## 4. The clock - `plan`, then `brief <cut>`

```bash
npx tsx pipeline/reel.mts plan <folder>
npx tsx pipeline/reel.mts brief <folder> short      # then, later, long
```

`plan` computes both cuts' timing plans: busy stretches get more frames and quiet ones fewer, with a hold
around each event. `brief` writes `<cut>/inputs/`. Make the Short first: it is the cheap test of whether
anyone watches. The long cut is the one that earns watch hours.

## 5. The page - STOP 2 after `proof`

Read `<cut>/inputs/CONTRACT.md`, `REEL.md`, `CUT.md`, `brief.json` and `data.sample.json` (the shape; the
real block is hundreds of kilobytes, do not read it). Write `<cut>/out/strip.html`:

- **You never type a number.** The page holds one empty `<script id="strip-data"
  type="application/json">{}</script>`; `proof` writes the issued table and plan into it and refuses a page
  whose block is anything else.
- The clock, the order of the rows and the event frames come from the plan. Do not re-sort and do not run a
  constant clock.
- Choose the chart form the story needs (a ranking is a bar race, a crossing is a line race, shares are a
  stream). A second reel in the same form must not look like a recolour of the first: change the device,
  not only the palette.
- SVG, canvas 2D, DOM, CSS 3D or WebGL. Frames are drawn on the GPU; three.js arrives through one empty
  `<script id="vendor-three"></script>` block that `proof` fills (`CUT.md` has the WebGL rules).

```bash
npx tsx pipeline/reel.mts proof <folder> short
```

`proof` also draws two frames both cold and after the forty frames before them, and refuses a page where
the two differ (`CUT.md`, "Move things by layout, not by layer"): that is the full render's seek gate, found
in a minute.

Then **open the twelve PNGs in `<cut>/proof/` and look at them** (Read each one you need). Check what no
gate can: is the leader obvious, is every card on screen near its own date, does anything overlap, is the
bottom of a vertical frame free. Fix the page and run `proof` again until the frames are right.

For a look at the motion before a full render, `shot <folder> short [--at <frame>] [--seconds <n>]` encodes
a few seconds from the middle to `<cut>/shot/shot.mp4` with one `still.png`. It runs no gate and moves no
stage.

**STOP 2.** Show the operator the proof folder and say what you checked and what you are unsure of.

## 6. The cut - STOP 3

```bash
npx tsx pipeline/reel.mts cut <folder> short
```

A 60 s Short is 1,800 frames and took 5 minutes on this box (measured 2026-11-17, a DOM bar race); a 4 min
long cut is 7,200 frames, so expect about 20. Run it detached (PowerShell `Start-Process`, never a Bash background job, which dies at 10
minutes) and never edit the page while it renders: the seek gate compares cold frames against the capture.

The gates are **enforced**: frames re-rendered cold and out of order must match the capture, text must hold
the legibility floor inside the safe box, and the picture must move. A failed gate leaves the files in
`<cut>/render/` to look at, exits 1 and does not mark the cut made. Fix the page; never loosen a gate.

**STOP 3.** Report `<cut>/render/strip.mp4` (and `final.mp4`, the cut with its bed, when a track is
attached), the contact sheet, the gate lines, the renderer and the render time. A cut with no track is silent
and not publishable. Keeping, publishing and scheduling are the operator's acts.

## Rules

- **No generation in the picture.** No image model, no video model. If a reel seems to need a picture, it
  needs a different chart form. The only vendor a reel may touch is Suno for its bed, on the operator's yes.
- **No number from memory.** Not in the table, not in an event, not in a title.
- **No logos, flags drawn from memory, or likenesses of real people.** Names as type, colour by entity.
- **Speculation is labelled.** A what-if shows the real line beside a line marked FICTION for its whole
  length, and its source line says so.
- **Never open the operator's store.** This CLI reads and writes one folder and nothing else.
- **Do not commit a reel folder.** `foundry-out/` is the operator's output, not the repo's.
- After a session that made a cut, add one dated line to `knowledge/reel/backlog.md` under the idea: the
  measured continuity numbers and the operator's verdict in their words.

## /reels - the four jobs behind the studio (spark reel-studio)

The `/reels` screen walks a reel without this CLI: one film (desktop 1920x1080 at 1x, 60-120 s; phone 1080x1920,
the same timeline rendered every second frame) under `<reel>/film/`. Each step is a kernel job launched
receipt-first by `lib/producers/reel.ts` `launchReelJob(kind, slug, {mode, token?, note?, notes?})`: no token
answers the receipt, the token confirms it, a reel with that kind's job still open answers `existing`, and a reel at
the wrong stage (`lib/reel/film.ts` `filmStageOf`) is refused in one line ("the film has no bed yet"). Ids are
`job:<kind>:<slug>:<n>`. `reelJobsOf(slug)` is the screen's read: the latest job per kind, the render's frame
progress, the last error verbatim.

| Job | Lane | Steps | Writes |
|---|---|---|---|
| `reel-data` | seat.text | `data` | one seat turn (web) from `pipeline/REEL-DATA-PROMPT.md` and the idea (plus the operator's send-back note): `table.json`, `continuity.json`, `SOURCES.md`, `reel.json` `tableSha` |
| `reel-author` | seat.text | `author` | one seat turn (no web) from `pipeline/REEL-FILM-PROMPT.md`, `knowledge/reel/look.md`, the table and the redo pins in time order, plus one fix turn on a failed check: `film/reel.html`, `film/reel-desktop.html`, `film/*.js`, `film/data/`; the declared `out/reel.json` frames become `film.frames`; the last pair moves to `film/history/` |
| `reel-render` | box.render | `stills`, `render:desktop`, `render:phone`, `thumbs` | `film/stills/<face>-NN.png` (8 a face), `film/film-desktop.mp4`, `film/film-phone.mp4` (ffprobe, sha, `film.renders`), `film/progress.json` while drawing, `film/thumbs/{d,p}-NN.jpg`, `film/poster.jpg` |
| `reel-release` | box.render | `mix:desktop`, `mix:phone`, `encode:desktop`, `encode:phone`, `vmaf` | `film/mix-*.mp4` (the bed; atempo 2 on the phone), `film/release-*.mp4` (2-pass x264 to 50 / 30 MB, `film.copies`), VMAF recorded on each copy (a reading, never a gate) |

- A seat turn is the operator's seat: an unpriced receipt line, the seat's report booked after it. A dry job's turns
  are stand-ins (a stand-in table, a stand-in film) and never enter the seat; renders and releases are local and
  free, and run the same dry or live. `GRAVITONE_DRY_ONLY=1` refuses a live launch.
- A resumed job never redoes booked work: a paid turn's `out/` is collected, a face whose master is in the state is
  adopted, a render that crashed is drawn again from frame 0 (three crashes in a row fail the job).
- The renderer is `pipeline/reel/film-render.mts` (the night tool as a module, on the GPU browser):
  `npx tsx pipeline/reel/film-render.mts <filmDir> <out.mp4> --page reel-desktop.html --width 1920 --height 1080 --from 1500 --frames 150`
  renders a 5 s look by hand (`--shared` names the shared data; default `<reels>/_shared/data`).
