## The app does not explain itself

**Delete every sentence whose subject is the app. Keep every sentence whose subject is the work.**

That is the law, and the test is literally the grammatical subject:

- *The app.* "This tab shows…", "read across a row to see…", "a cue is a span of film…", "kept so
  that a script written under the old rule can be spotted…", "drag any layer to move it". The screen
  narrating its own mechanism, its own layout, or how to operate it. **It goes.**
- *The work.* A beat, a claim, a cut reason, a gate detail and its quote, a price, a vendor, a model,
  a run id, a constraint, a real error message. **It stays, verbatim** — and shortening it is the
  same defect in the other direction.

This is not a style preference, it was a measurement. On 2026-09-08 seven routes carried 410 rendered
prose strings across 100 component files; five independent route audits found ~170 places where a
component was accompanied by a sentence explaining what the component already showed, and all five
then proposed the SAME handful of replacement shapes. What follows is how it stays that way.

### The vocabulary comes first, always

`@gt/ui` - `packages/ui/src/components/`, one directory per shape, mapped to its prototype source in
`packages/ui/src/components/INVENTORY.md` (read it before writing a surface): `hint · tally · piprow ·
stackbar · ghost · upstream · keycaps · stalebadge · provenance · tabrail · chip · errorline · …`.
Every component has a specimen on `/kit`.

The reason prose kept being the answer is that **prose was the only primitive available**. If a
sentence is the only way you can say something, that is evidence the vocabulary is missing a shape —
add the shape to `@gt/ui`, once, with its specimen, and say so. Do not hand-roll a second spelling of a
chip that already exists.

### A container with a slot for a paragraph will be handed a paragraph

Every time, by everyone. v1's `Deck` `sub` and four hand-rolled tab bars' `blurb` fields were not
places narration happened to land — they were the reason it was written. **Do not add an explanatory
slot to a shared component.** A tab that needs a paragraph is a tab with a bad label; the state that
paragraph described belongs on the tab as a `Tally`.

### The twelve-word rule, and what it is not

`Hint` is the most useful and most dangerous thing in the vocabulary: it will hold anything. Moving a
sixty-word explanation behind a glyph **hides** narration, it does not delete it.

> If a disclosure runs past roughly twelve words it is usually still the app narrating itself.
> Delete it instead.

Twelve is held by the type (`hint/words.ts`) and by the gate, which reads the same constant; past 25
words a disclosure fails outright — it is a paragraph wearing a glyph.

### The gate, and the two things it cannot do

`npm run check:narration` (in `npm run verify`) walks `app/`, `components/` and `packages/ui/src` and
guards the mechanical channels narration escapes through: long native `title=` attributes and `Hint`
length (`text=`, as `@gt/ui` spells it), each a ratchet at zero. `pipeline/check-narration.mjs` states
its own limits in its header:

1. **It cannot tell whether a sentence's subject is the app or the work.** That judgement is the
   whole law and no regex will hold it. The gate guards the escape hatches; the law is enforced by
   reading the diff.
2. **It cannot see a hole.** The one defect that survived the 2026-09 refactor was a `Ghost` drawn at
   ~0.12 effective alpha — invisible on this app's ink ground, every gate green, the accessibility
   channel correct. **Photographing the screen caught it.** (Since then a mark holds 3:1 and text
   4.5:1, measured by `tests/live/legibility.live.spec.ts` and `studio-routes.live`.)

So when you delete a paragraph, look at what is left:

```bash
GRAVITONE_PRODUCE_SIDE=fixture NEXT_PUBLIC_DEV_AUTH=1 NEXT_DIST_DIR=.next-cx GRAVITONE_STORE_PATH=<temp file> npx next dev -p 3007
CX_BASE=http://localhost:3007 node pipeline/cx-capture.mjs <screen-id> out.png --width 1920
```

`pipeline/cx-screens.mjs` names every screen (`landing`, `floor*`, `board*`, `ledger`, `runs`,
`projects`, `studio-*`, `part-*`, `library*`, `audio*`, `kit*`), and the legibility lane walks the same
table. **Open the PNG.** A deleted paragraph must leave a shape, not a gap.

### Accessibility is not narration

`aria-label`, `sr-only` and a `Hint`'s body are the non-visual channel. When a sentence becomes a
glyph, a colour or a position, the glyph still needs an accessible name — a shape only a sighted mouse
user can read is a regression, not a fix. Never delete a11y text to lower a word count.

### Three standing exemptions

- **Code comments keep their literary register.** This law is about rendered text only. When you
  delete UI prose that recorded a real reason, move the reason into a comment rather than losing it.
- **Real errors explain themselves in full.** An `ErrorLine` carrying a vendor's or the store's
  sentence verbatim is the product working, not the app narrating. So is a destructive confirm's
  statement of consequence.
- **`Keycaps`.** A keymap is a table, not prose: every row is a key and a verb.

## The article pipeline

`lib/articles/` (CLI `pipeline/article.mts`, skills `techwriter` for the topic dialogue and the nightly
`--loop`, `article-run` for one headless run) writes a technical post from a registry topic and, on a
human's approval on the Board (`/board/pkt:article:<runId>`; `/articles/<runId>` redirects there),
opens a PR in ai-registry, which lands the run's `blog` slot on the Calendar. Read `docs/articles.md`
first. Two rules an agent session must keep: a `run` spends the operator's Claude seat (use the stub
agent, `ARTICLES_AGENT_BIN`, for anything that is not the real thing), and `approve` is never an
agent's act: it pushes a branch and opens a PR in another repository.

## Reading `.ai/registry-map.json`

The ranking is lexical; you choose the governing subject. A pair's `score` counts shared
tokens between a context and a subject, so it can put a subject that does not apply above the
one that does (measured: for asset-library, `generated-music-acceptance` 389 and `agent-memory` 361
outrank `visual-style-locking` 348, the one that actually governs it). Read the context's code, pick the
subjects that fit, and do not record a clean `/conform` verdict from a subject you did not first
confirm applies. The map is generated; do not hand-edit it. It joins against `context-map.json`, so
regenerate it only after the owning machine's rescan has landed, and only for this repo:
`node ../ai-registry/scripts/build-registry-map.mjs --path <this checkout> --project gravitone` (run
bare it rewrites every sibling repo's map).

## Git policy (harness-enforced)

**Commit on the current branch; never push.** The owner pushes after reading the log. Do not push, force-push, or open a pull request unless this session's prompt asks for it. This line exists because the harness's own default for an unattended session is to push and open a draft PR when the file says nothing (measured 2026-09-08: without such a line the agent pushed; with it the agent stopped at the commit). The gate bypass is denied to agents at the permission layer (`.claude/settings.json` `permissions.deny`: `--no-verify`, force-push, `GRAVITONE_SKIP_GATE=1`) - do not work around it; if a gate is red, fix the tree.
