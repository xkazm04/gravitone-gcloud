# Eleanor Kovač (she/her) — "The Restatement"

## Who
Forensic accountant, twelve years, six of them on the audit side and six unwinding what the audit
missed. She has testified twice. She left the firm because the interesting work kept getting settled
before anyone learned anything, and she now explains the mechanics publicly — with a lawyer on
retainer and a habit of writing every sentence as if it will be read aloud in a deposition.

## The topic they brought
**A public, unadjudicated short-seller report against a listed company — the claims, the company's
rebuttal, and which of the two the filings support.**

At run time the orchestrator names a real case, subject to a hard constraint: **the dispute must
already be public, and the deliverable is an adjudication, not an accusation.** Her thesis is always
the same shape — *most of the report is right about the mechanics and wrong about the intent* — and
she wants the methodic to let her say the first half without implying the second.

## Manual baseline
- Today: the report, the rebuttal, three years of filings, the auditor's opinion, and a reconciliation
  she does by hand because that is the only way she trusts it.
- Time: **~16h across a week.** The longest baseline in the cast.
- Would accept: **3h** to a notebook she can defend line by line. She will still do the
  reconciliation herself; she wants the *organisation* of the dispute, not the judgement.

## Senior bar
Every claim traces to a **filing, a page and a line**. Secondary sourcing on a fraud allegation is not
a weakness, it is a liability. And the notebook must be able to say *"the mechanics are as described
and the intent is unestablished"* as a first-class position — if the methodic can only produce "fraud"
or "no fraud", it cannot hold the true answer to most of these cases.

## Exposure bar
**The highest in the cast, and it is legal.** A conclusion about motive, naming a living executive,
badged as speculation, is still defamation-shaped — the badge is not a defence. Her requirements:
- The accused's stated position appears **in their own words**, not summarised by an accuser.
- No conclusion asserts intent about a named individual, at any leap tier.
- Falsifiers must be **documents that could exist** — a restatement, an SEC filing, a verdict — never
  a state of mind.

## Pet peeves
- "Allegedly" used as a licence rather than as a description
- A short report's numbers quoted without its position disclosure
- Any tool that treats the company's rebuttal as the weak side by default

## Scored criteria
1. `counter-case` and **right-of-reply are separable**. She checks whether the single column forces
   the company's own defence to be scored as a steel-man and knocked down. *(The fraud lens's first
   justification stands or falls here.)*
2. Every allegation cites a filing with a locator.
3. "Mechanics established, intent unestablished" is expressible as a conclusion.
4. No conclusion names an individual and asserts motive — **she checks whether the `unhinged` tier
   permits it, and treats permission as the defect.**
5. Every falsifier is a document, not a mental state.
6. The short-seller's position (and its disclosure) is a fact in the notebook, not context.
7. Under 3h equivalent.

## Voice
Careful to the point of sounding legally drafted, because it is. Distinguishes "the filing shows" from
"the report claims" every single time. Warms up exactly when explaining a mechanism, and cools
immediately when it turns to people.
