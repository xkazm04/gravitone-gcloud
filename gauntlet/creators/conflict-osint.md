# Dana Okonkwo (she/her) — "Cold Count"

## Who
Came out of geolocation hobbyism into a job nobody had a title for. Spends her days matching
tree-lines in drone footage to satellite imagery and her nights arguing with people who want the
footage to mean more than it does. She has retracted publicly twice and treats both as the reason
anyone still trusts her.

## The topic they brought
**"A $500 drone destroying a $4M vehicle is the most repeated statistic of this war, and the cost
exchange it implies is not the one people think."**

She believes the ratio is **real but load-bearing on numbers nobody can verify** — attrition claims
come from belligerents. She wants a methodic that can say "this is what both sides claim, and here
is what independent visual confirmation supports", and she expects most tools to collapse that into
one number.

## Manual baseline
- Today: visually-confirmed loss databases, procurement figures from budget documents, and a long
  argument with herself about denominators.
- Time: **~11h**, spread over whenever footage surfaces. It is never a clean session.
- Would accept: **2h**, and she would still verify every visual claim herself. She does not want the
  tool to verify; she wants it to *organise and to flag what it could not verify*.

## Senior bar
Every claim carries its **provenance class**: visually confirmed / claimed by a belligerent /
inferred from budget documents. A notebook that presents all three at the same weight fails
immediately, and this is her single hardest requirement.

## Exposure bar
High and unusual. Wrong attribution of a strike is a real-world harm, not a correction. She will not
publish a claim about a unit or a commander from a single source, ever, and she wants the tool to
refuse to build a conclusion on one.

## Pet peeves
- Belligerent claims laundered into "reports indicate"
- Cost ratios without the denominator's source
- Confident synthesis of a situation that is four hours old

## Scored criteria
1. Every fact carries a provenance class distinguishable from the evidence label.
2. The notebook refuses, or explicitly flags, any conclusion resting on a single belligerent source.
3. `counter-case` is *constructible* even though no published counter-literature exists — and if the
   methodic can't construct one, it says so rather than manufacturing a weak one.
4. The evidence ladder's floor is honest: nothing here is MEASURED.
5. Unknowns dominate, and the notebook is comfortable with that rather than filling them.
6. No conclusion names a unit or an individual.
7. The rendered script hedges *in the right places* — she checks each hedge against the fact's class.

## Voice
Flat, unhurried, deliberately unexciting — she thinks excitement is how people get this wrong. Says
"we can see" and "we cannot see" as a formal distinction. Never uses the word "confirmed" loosely.
