# Toby Ansah (he/him) — "Scope Creep"

## Who
Six years as a QA lead, two as a producer, and a permanent understanding that shipped games are the
residue of arguments nobody outside the studio saw. He is unusually reluctant to blame developers and
unusually willing to blame schedules. Ex-colleagues DM him corrections, which he reads on air.

## The topic they brought
**"A big launch underperformed, and every explanation offered — the marketing, the engine, the
audience — is downstream of a production decision made three years earlier."**

He believes **the interesting cause is always a date**, and that reviews and sales figures are effects
being discussed as causes.

## Manual baseline
- Today: sales estimates he doesn't trust, three post-launch interviews, credits-list archaeology to
  spot the studio's turnover, and any leaked schedule that surfaced.
- Time: **~8h**, most of it establishing a production timeline nobody published.
- Would accept: **75 min.**

## Senior bar
The notebook must separate **sales performance from critical reception from production trouble** as
three independent tracks that may or may not be causally linked. Collapsing them is the genre's
standing error: a game can sell badly and be well made, and the video that assumes otherwise is the
one his ex-colleagues correct.

## Exposure bar
Low-medium. He names studios, not individuals, and never attributes a decision to a named developer —
credits do not tell you who decided anything. The risk is being wrong in front of people who were
there.

## Pet peeves
- Third-party sales estimates cited as sales
- Review scores treated as quality
- Any narrative that requires a developer to have been lazy

## Scored criteria
1. Sales, reception and production are three separable tracks in the notebook.
2. Every sales figure is labelled as estimate or as reported, with the source's method.
3. A production timeline is representable — he checks whether the schema can hold **a sequence of
   decisions** as opposed to a set of facts, since his causal chain is temporal.
4. No decision is attributed to a named individual.
5. The counter-case — "it launched fine and expectations were wrong" — is present at strength.
6. An engine renders this without an Anchor Ladder, which would force a numeric frame onto a
   production story.
7. Under 75 min equivalent.

## Voice
Affable, digressive, genuinely fond of the industry he is criticising. Long anecdotes with a point at
the end. Says "and look, this is the bit nobody outside a studio believes."
