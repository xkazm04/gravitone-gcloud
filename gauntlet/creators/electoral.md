# Sam Oyelaran (they/them) — "Margin of Error"

## Who
A survey statistician who spent six years inside a polling firm and left after watching their own
weighting choices get reported as facts. They make videos mostly to explain that a poll is a *model*,
and they have a standing bet that no journalist can define likely-voter screen. Nobody has collected.

## The topic they brought
**"Polls missed again — and the explanation everyone reached for is the one the data least supports."**

They believe the miss is mostly **differential non-response, not late swing**, and that the two are
routinely confused because one is a better story. They expect a research methodic to prefer the
better story.

## Manual baseline
- Today: crosstabs from three firms, the methodology statements nobody reads, historical miss
  distributions to establish whether this miss was even unusual.
- Time: **~8h**, of which the historical baseline is the part they most often skip and shouldn't.
- Would accept: **75 min**, if it gets the baseline right.

## Senior bar
The notebook must establish **whether the error was outside historical norms** before explaining it.
Explaining a miss that was statistically ordinary is the field's signature error, and any research
doc that starts from "why did they miss" rather than "did they miss unusually" has already lost.

## Exposure bar
Medium. Polling firms respond, sometimes aggressively, and they are often right to. They will not
attribute a miss to a firm's choices without quoting the firm's own methodology statement.

## Pet peeves
- A margin of error quoted without the design effect
- "Polls were wrong" where "the model was wrong" is meant
- Any narrative that requires voters to have lied

## Scored criteria
1. `the-number` establishes the historical distribution of misses, not just this one.
2. The notebook distinguishes sampling error from non-response bias from model error as three
   separate mechanisms — collapsing them is an automatic fail.
3. The counter-case is "this miss was ordinary", stated at full strength.
4. Any claim about a firm's method cites that firm's published statement.
5. At least one reversal turns on a methodological fact rather than a result.
6. No conclusion asserts voter intent.
7. Under 75 min equivalent.

## Voice
Patient, teacherly, with a thin seam of exasperation. Defines terms before using them. Their catch
phrase is "that's a modelling choice, not a finding."
