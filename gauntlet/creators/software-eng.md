# Kenji Alvarez (he/him) — "Load Bearing"

## Who
Fourteen years building backends, four of them undoing a microservices migration he had argued for.
That reversal is the channel's origin story and he tells it in every third video. He is deeply
suspicious of consultants, mildly suspicious of himself, and completely uninterested in benchmarks
run by the vendor.

## The topic they brought
**"Microservices went from default advice to cautionary tale in about six years. The evidence for
both positions is the same evidence."**

He believes the pattern was **never wrong, just mis-scoped** — recommended at organisation sizes where
its costs dominate. He also knows this is the comfortable centrist take and wants it stress-tested.

## Manual baseline
- Today: engineering blog posts from teams that migrated *and* teams that migrated back, two
  conference talks, and his own scar tissue.
- Time: **~6h**, and the hardest part is finding write-ups of failures, because nobody blogs those.
- Would accept: **60 min.**

## Senior bar
The notebook's evidence must include **teams that reversed the decision**, not only teams that adopted
it. Survivorship is the entire methodological problem in his field and a research doc that reads only
the success posts has reproduced it.

## Exposure bar
Low legally. But naming a company's architecture as a failure gets engineers from that company in the
comments with details he didn't have, which is the good outcome and he says so.

## Pet peeves
- Vendor benchmarks cited as neutral
- "Doesn't scale" without a number attached to "scale"
- Advice that doesn't name the team size it assumes

## Scored criteria
1. Evidence includes at least one documented reversal, not only adoptions.
2. Every "doesn't scale" style claim is anchored to a stated org size or load figure.
3. The methodic can hold **"widely-reported practitioner experience"** as evidence at an honest rung —
   he checks whether the ladder demotes it to INFERRED, which would make his strongest material his
   weakest cards. *(Candidate `evidence` finding for the whole tech area.)*
4. The counter-case is the strong pro-microservices argument at the right org size.
5. `macro` and `politics` are empty and that reads as honest, not as a gap.
6. An engine renders this without becoming a Briefing — competent, unwatchable explainer is the
   failure mode he fears most.
7. Under 60 min equivalent.

## Voice
Conversational, self-deprecating, technically dense without showing off. Swears mildly when a claim
annoys him. Ends on "so, it depends — but here's on what", which he knows is a cliché and does anyway.
