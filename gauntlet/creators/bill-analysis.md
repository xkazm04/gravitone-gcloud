# Priya Raghunathan (she/her) — "Read The Bill"

## Who
A regulatory lawyer who quit private practice because she could not stand watching commentary about
statutes written by people who had not opened them. Her format is a screen recording of the text with
her voice over it. Her audience is half compliance officers and half people who like watching someone
be precise.

## The topic they brought
**"The AI Act: which obligations actually bind you, which bind your vendor, and which do not exist
until 2027."**

She believes **most coverage conflates the tiers and the timelines**, and that the interesting story
is the gap between what people are preparing for and what the text says. She is not looking for a
scandal; she is looking for the four sentences that matter.

## Manual baseline
- Today: the consolidated text, the recitals, two implementing acts, and a table she rebuilds every
  time because nobody publishes it correctly.
- Time: **~7h**, most of it building the obligation-by-actor-by-date table.
- Would accept: **45 min** — and what she actually wants is the table, not prose.

## Senior bar
**Article-level citation or it does not exist.** A claim about what the law requires, sourced to a
press summary, is the failure mode of her entire field. She will reject a notebook that cites a
commentary where the text was available.

## Exposure bar
Medium-high, and specific: people make compliance decisions from her videos. A wrong date or a wrong
tier is not embarrassing, it is costly to a viewer. She adds a disclaimer to every video and still
worries.

## Pet peeves
- "The law says" followed by a paraphrase of a paraphrase
- Entry-into-force confused with entry-into-application
- Any tool that treats a recital as an obligation

## Scored criteria
1. Every legal claim cites an article or recital number, not a summary.
2. Obligations are separated by **actor** and by **date** — the notebook holds both axes or it is
   useless to her.
3. The distinction between binding text and interpretive guidance is preserved, not flattened.
4. `the-number` has no natural referent here — she checks whether the methodic forces one anyway.
5. The counter-case is a real competing legal reading, with its source.
6. No conclusion states a legal requirement; conclusions may only state consequences of the readings.
7. Under 45 min equivalent, or the table is good enough to justify longer.

## Voice
Exact, unhurried, faintly amused by imprecision. Reads text aloud and then says what it does not say.
Her highest compliment is "that's actually right."
