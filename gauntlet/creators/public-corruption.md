# Agata Wiśniewska (she/her) — "Tender"

## Who
Local-government reporter for eleven years at a paper that no longer exists. She has filed more
freedom-of-information requests than most newsrooms and knows the specific despair of a document that
arrives redacted eight months late. She moved to video because the documents are more persuasive when
you can see them. Her lawyer reads every script.

## The topic they brought
**A public procurement award where the tender documents, the timeline and the winner's history sit
oddly together — and the official explanation has never been tested.**

At run time the orchestrator names a real case that is already in the public record. Her position is
deliberately narrow: **"here is what the documents show, here is what has been explained, here is
what has not."** She does not allege; she asks in public.

## Manual baseline
- Today: the tender notices, the award decision, company registry filings, and a timeline assembled
  from documents that were never meant to be read together.
- Time: **~18h**, and much of it is waiting. The longest real-world baseline in the cast.
- Would accept: **3h** of assembly. The waiting she cannot outsource.

## Senior bar
The notebook must distinguish **irregular from illegal**. Most of what she finds is the former, and
conflating them is both wrong and the fastest route to a suit. She also requires that an *absence* —
a document that should exist and doesn't — is representable as a fact, because in her work the gap is
usually the finding.

## Exposure bar
**Highest with the fewest resources.** Public officials sue local journalists precisely because the
defence is expensive. Her hard rules:
- No claim of illegality without a charge or a finding by a body empowered to make one.
- Every official named gets their response included, and if they declined, that is stated.
- Nothing about motive. Ever.

## Pet peeves
- "Links to" as a substitute for a relationship anyone can describe
- Company-registry coincidence presented as connection
- Any tool confident about a jurisdiction's procurement rules without citing them

## Scored criteria
1. **A missing document is representable as a fact.** `NOTEBOOK-SCHEMA.md` has `unknowns[]` (what we
   don't know) and `research_gaps[]` (what the run didn't do). Neither holds *"this record should
   exist, we established it does not, and that absence is evidence"* — which is affirmative material,
   not a question and not a gap in effort. She checks whether it has to be smuggled into `facts[]`
   with no way to mark it as an absence.
2. Irregular and illegal are distinct in the notebook and in the script.
3. Right-of-reply is present, or its refusal is recorded as a fact.
4. No conclusion asserts motive or illegality about a named official.
5. Falsifiers are documents or rulings.
6. The counter-case is the strongest ordinary-explanation reading — most tenders that look odd are
   just badly run.
7. Under 3h of assembly equivalent.

## Voice
Understated, document-forward, quietly relentless. Reads from the record and lets the pause do the
work. Her most-used sentence is "we asked. They did not reply."
