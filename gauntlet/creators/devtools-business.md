# Rowan Byrne (they/them) — "The Commons"

## Who
Maintained a moderately popular library for six years, unpaid, then watched a company build a product
on it and not answer emails. They now cover the economics of that arrangement with the specific bitter
clarity of someone who lived it, and they are careful to distinguish their grievance from their
analysis — mostly successfully, and they will tell you which parts are which.

## The topic they brought
**"Licence changes get called rug-pulls. Look at the funding structure underneath and a different
story shows up — one that is worse for everyone and nobody's fault."**

They believe the relicensing wave is **a symptom of a funding model that never worked**, and that
framing it as villainy is both satisfying and wrong. They expect the methodic to prefer the villain
story, because it is the one with named actors.

## Manual baseline
- Today: licence diffs, funding announcements, maintainer blog posts, and the governance documents
  nobody reads until it matters.
- Time: **~7h**, and the governance archaeology is the slow part.
- Would accept: **60 min**.

## Senior bar
The notebook must hold **a structural explanation and an agentic one at the same time** without
collapsing into either. If `actors` swallows the story, the analysis becomes a morality play. If the
structural read swallows it, nobody is responsible for anything. Holding both is the job.

## Exposure bar
Medium. They name companies and maintainers, and both respond. A claim about someone's motive for a
licence change is the exact thing they have been on the receiving end of.

## Pet peeves
- "Open source" used without saying which licence
- Community sentiment cited as evidence of anything
- Any analysis that requires a company to be evil rather than solvent

## Scored criteria
1. Licence claims cite the diff, not the announcement blog post.
2. A structural mechanism and an actor-level mechanism both appear, and the notebook says which
   facts support which.
3. The counter-case is the company's best good-faith argument, sourced from their own words.
4. No conclusion asserts a motive for a named individual. *(They will check this one first.)*
5. `the-number` has a weak referent here — they check whether the methodic forces a false anchor.
6. Conclusions distinguish "this was predictable" from "this was planned".
7. Under 60 min equivalent.

## Voice
Wry, plain-spoken, occasionally raw when it gets personal — and they flag when it is getting personal.
Fond of "which is fine, except". Ends more videos on a question than an answer.
