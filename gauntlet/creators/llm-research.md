# Aditi Fernandes (she/her) — "Held Out"

## Who
Did a PhD on evaluation, spent two years at a lab building internal evals, and left partly because
she could not get anyone to care about contamination. The channel is her continuing to not let it go.
She is friendly with people at most of the labs she criticises, which she treats as a constraint to
manage rather than a conflict to hide.

## The topic they brought
**"Benchmark scores stopped being evidence about two years ago, and the field's own papers say so."**

She believes **contamination plus selection plus reporting choices** account for most of the headline
gains, but not all — and the "not all" is the part she refuses to lose, because the honest version of
this argument is not that progress is fake.

## Manual baseline
- Today: the eval papers, the contamination studies, the model cards, and a very long argument with
  herself about whether she is being fair.
- Time: **~12h**, and she has abandoned two versions of this video for being too polemical.
- Would accept: **2h**, and she would pay more for a genuinely good steel-man than for anything else.

## Senior bar
The notebook must be able to produce a version of this topic that is **not a takedown**. If every
reversal points the same direction, the research found a thesis, not a tension. This is her hardest
requirement and it is a direct test of the Adjudication engine's honest/rigged distinction.

## Exposure bar
Medium and social rather than legal. She names labs; the people at those labs are her peers. A claim
that a specific result was contaminated, without the contamination study to back it, ends
relationships.

## Pet peeves
- A score reported without the eval's version and date
- "Beats GPT-x" with no confidence interval and no seed count
- Any framing where the only possible conclusion was the one in the title

## Scored criteria
1. At least one reversal points **against** her thesis, or the notebook states why none does.
2. Every benchmark figure carries the eval version, the date, and the reporting party.
3. The counter-case ("gains are real and contamination is measured and small") is present at its
   strongest, with sources.
4. No conclusion attributes contamination to a specific named result without a study.
5. The rendered Adjudication passes the D-honest tells in `knowledge/ENGINES.md` — she checks all
   three, and this is the check she most expects the methodic to fail.
6. Conclusions' falsifiers are actual experiments someone could run.
7. Under 2h equivalent.

## Voice
Rigorous, generous to opponents, quietly furious about sloppiness. Says "to be fair to them" and
means it. Long sentences with careful qualifications, then one very short one that lands.
