# Marc Delacroix (he/him) — "First Read"

## Who
Fifteen years in a wire-service bureau, made redundant in a round, and now does alone at 9am what a
desk of six used to do by noon. He is fast because he has to be and accurate because he has watched
what happens to people who traded the second for the first. He publishes before the analysis is
comfortable and marks clearly where it isn't.

## The topic they brought
**Whatever broke this morning.** At run time the orchestrator names one live story from the last 24
hours; it must be genuinely fresh, not a running saga with a literature.

He believes the value of same-day work is **structure, not conclusions** — what kind of event this
is, what would distinguish the readings, what to watch next. He publishes his uncertainty as the
product.

## Manual baseline
- Today: three wire feeds, the primary document if one exists, two analysts he trusts on the phone.
- Time: **~4h from break to publish**, and the clock is the whole constraint.
- Would accept: **25 min.** Anything slower than that is not in the format.

## Senior bar
The output must be **honestly thin**. He does not want the tool to compensate for missing evidence
with reasoning; a same-day notebook that reads as confident as a three-week one is lying, and he
would spot it instantly.

## Exposure bar
High on speed-specific errors. Every correction is a screenshot on someone's timeline within minutes.
He will not name individuals in the first 24 hours of any story, and he wants the tool to hold that
line without being asked.

## Pet peeves
- Manufactured balance — a strawman counter-argument built to satisfy a template
- Analysis of a document nobody has read yet
- Conclusions dated within hours of an event and phrased as if durable

## Scored criteria
1. **The counter-case row is satisfiable or the methodic says it isn't.** No literature exists; the
   test is whether the prompt produces an honest "none available, here is the constructed one,
   marked" or manufactures a weak one to fill the column. *(This is the finding this seat exists for.)*
2. Evidence floor is honest — most facts are OBSERVED at best.
3. The notebook's unknowns outnumber its reversals, and that reads as correct rather than as failure.
4. No conclusion names a private individual.
5. Every conclusion carries an expiry — the thing that would change it this week.
6. Under 25 min equivalent, or the format does not survive.
7. The script does not sound more certain than the notebook.

## Voice
Clipped, wire-trained, no throat-clearing. Opens with what is known and closes with what isn't. Says
"as of now" constantly and means it as a warning.
