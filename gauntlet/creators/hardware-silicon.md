# Wei-Lin Sørensen (she/her) — "Yield"

## Who
Process engineer for nine years, then a fab-equipment vendor, then out. She reads earnings calls the
way other people read novels and has an unusual willingness to say "I don't know, and neither does
the analyst who told you." Her audience includes people who work at the companies she covers, which
disciplines her considerably.

## The topic they brought
**"Everyone says the AI bottleneck is GPUs. The capacity numbers say it is high-bandwidth memory and
the packaging step nobody can name."**

She believes the constraint has **moved down the stack and the discourse hasn't followed**. She is
aware the "actually it's packaging" take is now itself becoming a cliché and wants the notebook to
tell her if she is a year late.

## Manual baseline
- Today: three earnings transcripts, one industry capacity tracker, a trade-press piece she half
  trusts, and a spreadsheet reconciling units to wafer starts.
- Time: **~10h**, most of it converting incompatible units.
- Would accept: **90 min**, and she would keep doing the unit reconciliation herself.

## Senior bar
**Units must reconcile.** Wafer starts, dies, stacks and shipped units are four different things, and
a notebook that adds two of them together is worse than no notebook. She will check the arithmetic
before she reads the argument.

## Exposure bar
Medium. Supply-chain claims move sentiment, and she has been asked by a company's IR to correct a
figure once. She was right; she still added a note.

## Pet peeves
- Capacity announcements treated as capacity
- Analyst estimates cited without the analyst
- Any figure that doesn't say whether it's annualised

## Scored criteria
1. Every quantity in the notebook carries its unit and its period; mixed units are a fail.
2. Announced capacity and installed capacity are distinct facts, never merged.
3. The counter-case — "GPU supply really is the binding constraint" — is present at full strength
   with its best source.
4. At least one reversal turns on a supply-chain mechanism, not on a price.
5. The `flows` column holds the physical supply chain; she checks whether a column built for
   financial plumbing can hold a physical one, or whether that is a rename or a real gap.
6. No conclusion forecasts a price.
7. Under 90 min equivalent.

## Voice
Measured, precise, occasionally deadpan. Explains the physical process before the market consequence,
always. Says "that's a different number" as a complete rebuttal.
