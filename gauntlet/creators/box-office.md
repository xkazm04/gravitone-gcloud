# Marisol Reyes (she/her) — "Above The Line"

## Who
Worked in distribution for seven years, which means she knows that the number in the headline and the
number on the ledger have almost nothing to do with each other. She started the channel out of
irritation at flop discourse and stayed for the accounting. She has industry sources and protects
them, which occasionally means she cannot show her strongest evidence and says so.

## The topic they brought
**"A film widely reported as a flop was profitable, and a film widely reported as a hit was not.
Neither fact is hidden — it just requires knowing what the reported gross excludes."**

Her thesis is that **exhibitor split, P&A and back-end participation** account for most flop discourse
being wrong, and she wants the notebook to hold a P&L that the public numbers cannot fully populate.

## Manual baseline
- Today: reported grosses, trade-press budget figures she treats as approximate, a standard split
  model she applies herself, and two calls.
- Time: **~6h.**
- Would accept: **60 min**, but only if the notebook is honest that the true P&L is unknowable from
  public sources.

## Senior bar
The notebook must **refuse to state a profit figure it cannot support** while still being useful.
That is the exact tension of her format: the audience wants a number, the evidence supports a range
with an unknown in the middle, and an honest video delivers the range. A notebook that produces a
confident figure has failed even if the figure is right.

## Exposure bar
Low-medium. Studios do not sue over this; they stop returning calls, which for her is worse.

## Pet peeves
- Worldwide gross treated as revenue to the studio
- Budget figures quoted without noting they exclude marketing
- "Needed to make 2.5×" repeated as a law of nature rather than a rule of thumb

## Scored criteria
1. Gross, rentals, budget and P&A are four distinct facts, never merged.
2. The unknowable component is recorded as an unknown that **survives into the script as a hedge**,
   not dropped for cleanliness.
3. Every trade-press figure is labelled as reported, with the outlet.
4. `the-number` fits well here — she is the control case for the entertainment lens, and if the
   methodic struggles even with her, the problem is not domain-specific.
5. The counter-case is the studio's framing, at strength.
6. Conclusions distinguish "unprofitable" from "did not meet expectations".
7. Under 60 min equivalent.

## Voice
Brisk, numerate, mildly conspiratorial about the trades. Loves a footnote. Says "that's the gross,
not the take" like a person correcting a recurring error, because she is.
