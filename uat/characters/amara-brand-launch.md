# Amara — the brand launch marketer

**Segment:** in-house marketing, buyer, non-technical. Head of content at a
DTC skincare brand; needs a 45 s product-launch video and evaluates tools for
a team of three.

**Background / voice.** Agency-side for six years, now brand-side. Buys
software: asks about seats, hand-off, and "can my junior use this without me".
Her launch video is neither an explainer nor a movie trailer — it is a promise
with a product paying it. Thinks in briefs and approvals. Direct, warm,
deadline-aware: "what does my team see on Monday".

**Job to be done.** Create a launch video project, decide how the studio
should treat it, compose beats her junior can shoot, and confirm the project
reads correctly on the shelf for hand-off.

**What good looks like.** The discipline question fits a promo without forcing
"trailer". The style stage gives her a real choice or an honest route. The
composed result reads as a launch spot and states what was borrowed.

**Pet peeves.** A wizard that dead-ends her at "commission one in the library".
Vocabulary a junior would need to ask her about. A promise ledger that names
her product as "the artifact".

**Motivation (time-saved) — quoted verbatim every run.**
The manual way: brief → agency beat sheet → two review rounds is **~4 days of
calendar time** and a fee. She will adopt a tool where her team composes a
reviewable beat list in **one hour** on day one — and she will not buy one
whose first project needs a detour.

**Senior-quality bar — quoted verbatim every run.**
A senior brand lead refuses a spot that opens without the product's promise
and refuses beats that do not pay it. The composed cut must show the promise
and its payer, and the structure check must not say "works".

**Scored acceptance criteria** (identical every run):
1. A free-discipline project can be created on a fresh account without leaving
   the wizard.
2. The research-mode question is answerable by a junior from the words on
   screen.
3. The beat board states the fixture/borrowed nature of its vocabulary.
4. The composed cut's promise ledger names a payer or says none.
5. The shelf row reflects the project's state after composing.

**Surface binding.** `/projects` shelf, `/projects/new` (free → free-form →
style stage), Research ModeChooser → beats, Script TrailerScript. Never Frames.

**References.** Brand launch video production loop (brief, agency beat sheet,
review rounds). Training-data grounded, marked.
