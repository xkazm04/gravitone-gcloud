# Nadia — the essayist

**Segment:** solo creator, technical enough. Writes, narrates and edits her own
8–14 minute explainers. ~40k subscribers.

**Background / voice.** Came from financial journalism, left because the desk
wanted takes faster than she could source them. Her whole channel is built on
"I will show you the thing that changed my mind." She has been burned by an AI
writing tool that produced a confident paragraph citing a statistic that did not
exist — she caught it in the edit, and it is the reason she reads every line
before it is spoken. Talks in specifics; hates being sold to.

**Job to be done.** Take a researched notebook and get to a script whose argument
survives her own scrutiny — then adjust the balance when a section is running
long or a point is under-served, without losing the chain that makes it work.

**What good looks like.** After a rebalance, the script still *argues*. The turns
still land in the right order, the counter-case is still there, and nothing is
asserted that the notebook does not support.

**Pet peeves.** A tool that changes something and does not tell her what it
changed. A "confidence" score with nothing behind it. Being asked to trust a
regenerated draft that was not checked the way the first one was.

**Motivation (time-saved) — quoted verbatim every run.**
The manual way: rebalancing a 5-minute script by hand — re-reading the draft,
deciding what to cut, rewriting the transitions the cut breaks — is **90–120
minutes**, and she does it 2–3 times per video. The tool must get a rebalance
to a reviewable state in **under 10 minutes of her attention**, or she will keep
doing it in her document.

**Senior-quality bar — quoted verbatim every run.**
The rebalanced script must be at least as defensible as the one she would write
as a senior financial journalist: every load-bearing claim sourced, the
strongest counter-argument still present and argued at full strength, and no
causal claim where the evidence is only correlation. **A rebalance that improves
the pacing and quietly weakens the argument is a failure, not a trade-off.**

**Scored acceptance criteria** (identical every run):
1. After a rebalance, she can see *what changed* without diffing by eye.
2. The steel-man / counter-case survives, or its loss is refused or flagged.
3. No claim gains screen time that the notebook does not support.
4. A rebalance that no longer fits the runtime tells her so.
5. She can reject the result and keep her feedback.

**Surface binding.** Step 1 Research (triage board, notebook, evidence log),
Step 2 Script (all four tabs, the pad). Does not use Frames/Motion/Score/Cut.
