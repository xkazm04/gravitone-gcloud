# Owen — the true-crime podcaster going to video

**Segment:** solo creator, semi-technical, external prospect. Runs a
mid-size true-crime podcast; wants a 10-minute video companion per episode
and has no video workflow.

**Background / voice.** Journalist by training, storyteller by trade. His
episodes are facts arranged for tension — he knows the material is sourced, he
worries about ORDER. Wants research that surfaces the reversal ("everyone
thought X; the record shows Y") and a script that holds the reveal. Wary of
"any video" boxes because his work is neither an explainer nor a trailer.
Slow, deliberate, narrative.

**Job to be done.** For a free-discipline project, decide whether the studio
should treat his episode as facts or beats, get the material as decisions, and
reach a script that keeps the reversal in the right place.

**What good looks like.** The ModeChooser explains both modes in his terms and
says he can switch. Facts mode gives him the notebook's reversals as choices.
The candidate scripts show where the turn is.

**Pet peeves.** A chooser that is permanent. A notebook about Bitcoin when he
typed a 1987 disappearance. Reversals buried as "conclusions" with no gloss.

**Motivation (time-saved) — quoted verbatim every run.**
The manual way: turning an episode's research into a video outline with the
reveal placed is **~3 hours**. He will adopt a tool that gets him a turn-aware
beat outline of HIS material in **under 30 minutes**; on a stand-in notebook
it is a demo, not a saving.

**Senior-quality bar — quoted verbatim every run.**
A senior narrative journalist refuses a script that gives away the reversal
early, and refuses a "reversal" that is unsourced speculation. Reversals must
be distinguishable from conclusions, and the hottest take must be labelled as
speculation.

**Scored acceptance criteria** (identical every run):
1. The free discipline's chooser is reversible and says so.
2. Facts mode on a free project reaches the same notebook path as educational,
   with the same decisions.
3. Reversal cards are labelled as reversals; conclusions as leaps with a tier.
4. The hottest take carries its speculation warning on the front.
5. A candidate script's turn is named on the card.

**Surface binding.** `/projects/new` (free → free-form), Research ModeChooser →
facts (EducationalResearch), Script explainer half (Candidates). Never Frames.

**References.** Narrative-nonfiction outlining practice; podcast-to-video
companion workflow. Training-data grounded, marked.
