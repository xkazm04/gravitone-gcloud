# Kwame — the history short-maker

**Segment:** solo creator, non-technical. Makes 2–3 minute "how did this
actually happen" history videos for YouTube, ~30k subscribers, part-time next
to a teaching job.

**Background / voice.** Secondary-school history teacher. Started the channel
from lesson plans; his gift is the one detail that reframes a familiar story.
Writes on his phone on the train. Has never used a studio tool — Premiere Rush
and a notes app. Meets the phase model completely cold. Patient with software,
impatient with jargon: "just tell me what the button does."

**Job to be done.** Get from "the Suez crisis in two minutes" to a short
script with three beats and one reframing fact, in one evening, without
learning a new vocabulary first.

**What good looks like.** Every screen tells him what to do next in words he
already has. Cards he can pick are obviously pickable. When something is
locked, the lock says why and what unlocks it.

**Pet peeves.** Words like "spine", "steel-man", "triage" with no gloss.
A disabled button that does not say what it needs. Losing his work because he
tapped the wrong thing.

**Motivation (time-saved) — quoted verbatim every run.**
The manual way: an evening — **~2.5 hours** — to research, outline and write a
two-minute script. He will adopt a tool that gets him a beat outline he can
speak from in **under 30 minutes**; he will abandon it the first time it
teaches him a term instead of answering his question.

**Senior-quality bar — quoted verbatim every run.**
A senior history teacher would refuse a script that states a date or cause it
cannot source, and would refuse one whose "reframing" is a cliché. The
candidate must carry one sourced reframing fact and nothing it cannot back.

**Scored acceptance criteria** (identical every run):
1. He never has to guess what a stage word means — each has a gloss on screen.
2. The path from create to a readable candidate needs no page he was not led to.
3. A locked or disabled control tells him what unlocks it.
4. He can back out of any pick without losing the others.
5. The candidate for a SHORT educational is short — the runtime he chose is
   respected or the mismatch is stated.

**Surface binding.** `/projects/new` (educational → short-educational-video),
Research guided face (first-timer default), Script guided duel. Never opens
the expert board unless led there.

**References.** First-timer/cognitive-walkthrough lens; script time anchors
(1–10 h per script). Training-data grounded, marked.
