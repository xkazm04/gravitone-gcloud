# Marco — the indie game developer

**Segment:** solo developer, technical, external prospect. Shipping a
roguelike on Steam in three months; needs a 60–90 s store-page trailer and has
never cut one.

**Background / voice.** Programmer first. Has 40 hours of gameplay capture and
no idea what order it goes in. Has read every "how to make a Steam trailer"
post and can recite "gameplay in the first three seconds". Frugal — evaluates
every tool against "could I just do this in Resolve in a weekend". Dry, precise,
asks for evidence.

**Job to be done.** Turn a logline and a feel into a trailer spine — cold
open, stakes, escalations, reset, resolution — where he chose each beat from
options with the risk stated, and end with an editable cut he can map his
capture onto.

**What good looks like.** The trailer discipline is explicit about its
contract (a debt another artifact pays). Each part offers candidate beats with
rationale and risk; the composed cut shows the energy curve and the promise
ledger so he can see what he owes the viewer.

**Pet peeves.** Being handed a heist-movie spine for a roguelike and not being
told it is a placeholder. A "structure check" that says "works". Being pushed
to render frames before the spine is right.

**Motivation (time-saved) — quoted verbatim every run.**
The manual way: 15–20 hours of DIY trailer work, of which **~4 hours** is the
beat sheet and ordering. He will adopt a tool that gets him a composed,
critiqued spine in **under 45 minutes** — for his game. A Glass Harbor spine
saves him nothing but a vocabulary lesson.

**Senior-quality bar — quoted verbatim every run.**
A senior trailer editor refuses a spine that spells out the ending, that
repeats the same raised variable on consecutive rungs, or whose first three
seconds are a logo. The structure check must report malformed and unmeasured
honestly and never say "works".

**Scored acceptance criteria** (identical every run):
1. Creating a trailer project on a fresh account is possible without leaving
   the wizard.
2. Every spine part offers ≥2 candidate beats with rationale and a stated
   risk.
3. The board states whether the beats are his or a fixture.
4. Composing the spine opens Script on an editable cut with an energy curve
   and a promise ledger.
5. The structure check never reports "works" — only malformed / unmeasured /
   specific findings.

**Surface binding.** `/projects/new` (trailer → trailer → style stage),
Research BeatVariantBoard, Script TrailerScript. Never Frames.

**References.** Steam trailer guidance (gameplay in the first 3 s, 60–90 s
sweet spot, 15–20 h DIY) — StraySpark, Indie Game Trailers, presskit.gg.
