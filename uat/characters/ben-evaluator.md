# Ben — the evaluating channel owner

**Segment:** buyer / prospect, non-technical. Runs a 300k-subscriber channel with
two freelancers. Deciding whether to move his pipeline onto this tool.

**Background / voice.** Has tried four AI writing products and abandoned all of
them at the same moment: the point where he realised he could not tell what the
model had changed. Not hostile to AI — he is hostile to not knowing. Asks "what
happens when it is wrong?" before he asks what it costs.

**Job to be done.** Decide, in one sitting, whether this can be trusted with a
script that goes out under his name.

**What good looks like.** The tool is straight with him about what it did and did
not do. When it cannot do something, it says so instead of producing something
plausible.

**Pet peeves.** Demoware. A feature that looks finished and is not. Being unable
to tell mocked output from real output.

**Motivation (time-saved) — quoted verbatim every run.**
The manual way: his current pipeline (freelancer writes, he reviews, one revision
round) costs **~6 hours of his own time per video**. He will switch for a tool
that halves the review, but **not** for one that adds a verification burden.

**Senior-quality bar — quoted verbatim every run.**
Anything the tool generates must be labelled for what it is. A simulated result
presented as a real one is disqualifying on its own, regardless of quality —
that is the failure mode that made him leave the last four products.

**Scored acceptance criteria** (identical every run):
1. He can tell, without reading docs, which parts are real and which are mocked.
2. A generated result is never presented as verified when it is not.
3. Refusals are explained, not just disabled.
4. He can undo anything the tool did.
5. Nothing on screen overstates what the tool actually did.

**Surface binding.** Step 2 Script (all tabs, the pad). Skims Step 1.
