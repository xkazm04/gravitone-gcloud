# Ravi — the comedy sketch creator

**Segment:** solo creator, non-technical. Writes and performs 20–60 second
comedic sketches for Shorts/TikTok; ~80k followers; three sketches a week.

**Background / voice.** Improv background, day job in customer support (his
material). Thinks in setups and turns: premise → escalation → button. Has never
"researched" a video in his life; his prep is a list of beats on a napkin.
Allergic to tools that assume he is making an explainer. Fast, funny, blunt:
"where's the joke in this?"

**Job to be done.** Pick a comedic premise, get escalating beats laid out as
options he can choose between, and end with a beat list he can shoot from —
without being asked for facts, sources or a "topic to investigate".

**What good looks like.** The "any video" path asks him what kind of research
he wants in plain words, offers beats when he says beats, and the beats he
picks compose into something he can read top to bottom.

**Pet peeves.** Being asked to "research" a joke. A "trailer spine" vocabulary
(cold open, escalation, reset) that is close to his but not his — and not
saying it is borrowed. Beats that are about a heist movie when his sketch is
about a call centre.

**Motivation (time-saved) — quoted verbatim every run.**
The manual way: a sketch's beat list is **~30 minutes** on a napkin, and he
throws two of three away. He will adopt a tool that gives him three escalation
options per beat in **under 10 minutes** — for HIS premise. For a fixture
premise it saves nothing and costs him the reading.

**Senior-quality bar — quoted verbatim every run.**
A senior sketch writer refuses beats that do not escalate and refuses a button
that does not pay the premise. The beat candidates must each raise something
(scale, speed, threat, intimacy, cost), name what they raise, and the composed
list must end on a payoff.

**Scored acceptance criteria** (identical every run):
1. The free discipline's research-mode question is answerable without knowing
   the studio's words.
2. Beats are offered as pickable options with their downside stated on the
   tile.
3. The board says plainly whether the beats are about his premise or a
   stand-in.
4. He can switch facts ⇄ beats without losing picks.
5. The composed spine opens Script on something he can read as a sketch, not a
   trailer.

**Surface binding.** `/projects/new` (free → free-form → style stage), Research
ModeChooser → BeatVariantBoard, Script trailer half (the composed cut). Never
Frames.

**References.** Shorts story arc hook/build/payoff; sketch beat-listing
practice (Skillshare / sketch-writing guides). Training-data grounded, marked.
