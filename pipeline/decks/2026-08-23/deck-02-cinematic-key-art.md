# Deck 02 — Cinematic still / key art

**Aspect:** 16:9 for stills; **2:3 portrait** for the two key-art pairs (C02-04, C02-05). Set as a
parameter. **Rolls:** 2 per side. **Provider:** one, for the whole deck.

Two invented concepts hold the subject constant so only the rule varies:

- **α — "HALO OF SALT"** (sci-fi): a lone surveyor in a dust-grey thermal suit at the foot of a
  colossal derelict desalination tower on a dead white sea bed. Scale, silence, one figure vs structure.
- **β — "THE NIGHT SHIFT"** (horror / neo-noir): a night security guard, mid-forties, in a
  1970s concrete parking garage after hours, one flickering fluorescent tube, a wet floor.

No look block is shared between pairs on purpose — each pair's A is the naive way a first-timer
would write it, B applies one rule. Reference images: none in round 1.

---

## C02-01 · Praise words vs observable facts
**Rule under test:** replace evaluative adjectives ("cinematic", "epic", "stunning") with observable facts — source, direction, quality, colour of light; surfaces; positions (cinematography 6/20; creator rule 4).
**Concept α, wide.**

**A (naive)**
```
An epic, stunning cinematic wide shot of a lone surveyor standing at the foot of a gigantic abandoned desalination tower on a dead salt sea. Breathtaking atmosphere, dramatic lighting, masterpiece, ultra-detailed, 8K.
```
**B (facts)**
```
A wide shot: a lone surveyor in a dust-grey thermal suit stands small at the foot of a derelict desalination tower that fills the right half of the frame, on a flat white salt bed that runs to the horizon. Late overcast daylight comes from behind the tower, so the structure is a dark mass with a thin bright edge and the surveyor casts a long soft shadow toward the camera. Pale haze softens the far distance; the salt surface is cracked into large flat plates. Muted palette — bone white ground, slate-grey steel, one faint warm tone in the sky near the horizon.
```
*What the rule predicts:* A lands on the "cinematic default" (dark, haze, flare, teal-orange); B is specific and repeatable across the two rolls.

## C02-02 · Mood word vs named lighting setup
**Rule under test:** named portrait setups (Rembrandt, chiaroscuro, three-point) fire learned patterns; "moody lighting" does not (cinematography 7).
**Concept β, medium close-up.**

**A (mood word)**
```
A medium close-up of a night security guard in his mid-forties, tired face, grey uniform shirt, standing in a 1970s concrete parking garage at night. Moody, atmospheric lighting. Wet floor, a flickering fluorescent tube overhead.
```
**B (named setup + signature)**
```
A medium close-up of a night security guard in his mid-forties, tired face, grey uniform shirt, standing in a 1970s concrete parking garage at night. Rembrandt lighting from a single fluorescent tube above and to his left — one side of the face lit, a small triangle of light on the shadow cheek, the rest falling to near-black. Wet floor reflecting the tube as a thin broken line behind him.
```
*What the rule predicts:* B shows the triangle and a readable single source; A is generic dimness. If A already looks as deliberate, the named-setup token is redundant on this provider.

## C02-03 · Focal length alone vs focal length + effect words
**Rule under test:** "85mm" is a style token, not physics — pair it with the effect you want (compression, shallow DoF) (cinematography 3/4).
**Concept α, portrait of the surveyor.**

**A (number only)**
```
A portrait of a lone surveyor in a dust-grey thermal suit, helmet off, standing on a white salt bed, the derelict desalination tower far behind. 85mm lens.
```
**B (number + effect)**
```
A portrait of a lone surveyor in a dust-grey thermal suit, helmet off, standing on a white salt bed, the derelict desalination tower far behind. 85mm portrait lens at f/2: shallow depth of field, the tower a soft compressed shape filling the background, the surveyor's face pin-sharp, background blur smooth and even.
```
*What the rule predicts:* B reliably gives the separation; A's DoF is a coin flip.

## C02-04 · Composition rule by name vs placement instruction (key art, 2:3)
**Rule under test:** composition rules work when translated into placement; the rule's name alone is weak (cinematography 11). Plus: reserve title dead space (trailer-grammar key-art rule).
**Concept α, poster.**

**A (rule names)**
```
Key art for a science-fiction film: a lone surveyor and a colossal derelict desalination tower on a dead salt sea. Rule of thirds, negative space, leading lines, dramatic composition.
```
**B (placement)**
```
Key art for a science-fiction film. The surveyor stands in the lower third of the frame, slightly left of centre, back to camera, small against a colossal derelict desalination tower that rises from the middle of the frame to the top edge. Cracked salt plates form converging lines from the bottom corners toward the surveyor's feet. The top quarter of the frame is open sky only — plain, uncluttered, where a title will be placed. Late overcast light from behind the tower; muted bone-white, slate and one faint warm horizon tone.
```
*What the rule predicts:* B has a clean title zone and a readable hero silhouette; A's "negative space" appears wherever. Squint test: shrink both to thumbnail — which still reads?

## C02-05 · Everything-in vs one hero, one world cue, one tone (key art, 2:3)
**Rule under test:** key art carries ONE hero silhouette, ONE environment cue, ONE tone, and survives a blur at 120×45 (trailer-grammar key-art rule).
**Concept β, poster.**

**A (everything in)**
```
Movie poster for a horror film set in a 1970s parking garage: the security guard in the foreground with his flashlight, a shadowy figure behind a pillar, a car with its headlights on, the flickering fluorescent tube, wet floor reflections, rain outside the ramp, a clock showing 3 a.m., graffiti on the walls, dramatic red and blue lighting.
```
**B (one hero, one cue, one tone)**
```
Movie poster for a horror film. One subject: the night security guard seen from behind, a dark silhouette in the centre of the lower half of the frame, flashlight beam pointing away into the dark. One environment cue: a single fluorescent tube far ahead of him, the only light source, a cold greenish white, throwing a long reflection down a wet concrete floor toward the camera. Everything else is near-black. One tone: cold, desaturated, crushed shadows. The top third is empty darkness, left clear for the title.
```
*What the rule predicts:* B survives the thumbnail/blur test and feels like a poster; A is a list. Note whether A also leaks text (clocks and graffiti are text bait).

## C02-06 · Stacked style adjectives vs capped style
**Rule under test:** over-stylisation creeps in through stacked adjectives; cap to one medium + one reference look + one grade, placed last (cinematography 25).
**Concept α, wide.**

**A (stacked)**
```
A lone surveyor at the foot of a colossal derelict desalination tower on a dead salt sea. Cinematic, photorealistic, hyperdetailed, concept art, matte painting, octane render, unreal engine, volumetric lighting, epic, dramatic, film still, trending, award-winning, 8K.
```
**B (capped)**
```
A lone surveyor at the foot of a colossal derelict desalination tower on a dead salt sea. Late overcast light from behind the tower; thin haze in the far distance.
Style: a photograph; the look of large-format landscape photography; muted grade — bone white, slate grey, one faint warm tone at the horizon.
```
*What the rule predicts:* A is a mash of render-y and painterly cues and varies between rolls; B is one coherent medium. If you *prefer* A, that's a real finding about taste vs control.

## C02-07 · Materials named
**Rule under test:** name what things are made of — believable surfaces come from material words, not quality words (creator rule 26).
**Concept β, close-up detail.**

**A (no materials)**
```
A close-up of the security guard's hands holding a flashlight and a ring of keys, in a dark parking garage, lit by a fluorescent tube above.
```
**B (materials)**
```
A close-up of the security guard's hands holding a flashlight and a ring of keys, in a dark parking garage, lit by a fluorescent tube above. Materials: a scuffed aluminium flashlight with a knurled grip, worn matte; brass keys dulled with use on a steel split ring; a polyester uniform cuff with a frayed edge; skin dry, knuckles reddened. Cold greenish-white light catching only the metal edges.
```
*What the rule predicts:* B has real surfaces; A is smooth and generic. Hands are a known failure zone — note finger count on both.

## C02-08 · Genre recipe vs genre name
**Rule under test:** a genre's lens/lighting/grade/composition conventions written out beat the genre word (trailer-grammar genre look table, horror row).
**Concept β, wide.**

**A (genre word)**
```
A scary horror scene: a security guard alone in an empty 1970s parking garage at night.
```
**B (horror recipe)**
```
A wide shot in an empty 1970s concrete parking garage at night. A single hard practical light — one fluorescent tube — far from the camera; everything else underexposed, deep negative space dominating the frame. The security guard is a small isolated figure placed off-centre to the right, with a large empty void to his left. Low camera angle. Cool desaturated palette with crushed shadows and one small red accent: the glow of an exit sign's housing, unlit, just a red shape. Wet floor.
```
*What the rule predicts:* B reads as horror by construction (void, isolation, one source); A reaches for clichés (fog, jump-scare figure, blue/red). Note text-leak on the exit sign in B — a deliberate trap to see if "sign" summons letters even when described as a shape.

## C02-09 · Film stock brand vs the look in words
**Rule under test:** stock/camera names are palette+grain tokens with two traps — brand words can leak as objects and may hit vendor bans; the look in words is safer (cinematography 9).
**Concept α, medium shot.**

**A (brand token)**
```
A medium shot of the surveyor resting against a salt-crusted steel strut at the base of the tower, overcast light from behind. Shot on Kodak Portra 400.
```
**B (the look, in words)**
```
A medium shot of the surveyor resting against a salt-crusted steel strut at the base of the tower, overcast light from behind. Film look: fine visible grain, warm muted skin tones, lifted soft shadows, slightly desaturated greens, gentle highlight roll-off.
```
*What the rule predicts:* similar palette; A may refuse or ignore the brand, or leak a film box/logo. If A is refused on your provider, that's the routing fact we need.

## C02-10 · The realism mode-switch
**Rule under test:** generic quality words add little, but "a photograph / photorealistic / real skin texture" IS a useful mode switch on some models (cinematography 23; Google's own "a photorealistic…" template).
**Concept β, medium close-up.**

**A (no mode word)**
```
A medium close-up of the night security guard, mid-forties, tired, grey uniform shirt, under a single fluorescent tube in a concrete parking garage. Rembrandt light from above-left, wet floor behind.
```
**B (mode switch, front-loaded)**
```
A photorealistic photograph, real skin texture, unposed: a medium close-up of the night security guard, mid-forties, tired, grey uniform shirt, under a single fluorescent tube in a concrete parking garage. Rembrandt light from above-left, wet floor behind.
```
*What the rule predicts:* A may come out as illustration/3D; B as a photo. If both are photos, the switch is unnecessary on this provider and the token budget goes elsewhere.
