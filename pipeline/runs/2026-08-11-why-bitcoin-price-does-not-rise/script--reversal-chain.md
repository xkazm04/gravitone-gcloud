> So institutional adoption failed?

> No. It succeeded completely. **That is what did this.**
>
> ⟨mechanism — the thesis⟩ When institutions buy an asset, that asset goes into portfolios that are
> governed by risk models. It gets sized. It gets hedged. It gets rebalanced. It stops being a
> conviction and starts being a position — and positions get cut when the environment turns.
>
> So as Treasury yields climbed toward four and a half percent, and thirty-year yields touched five,
> Bitcoin moved with them — downward. Not because a bond yield reaches into a blockchain, but because
> the people who now own both are rebalancing one book. Nobody stopped believing in it. They just
> stopped being the only kind of person holding it.
>
> ⟨scale conversion⟩ Bitcoin now trades with a correlation to the Nasdaq of roughly zero point seven
> to zero point eight. On most days, it is a technology stock with extra steps. And it moves on the
> ten-year Treasury yield — the single most boring number in finance.

### REFRAME CLOSE · 4:45–5:00

> Bitcoin spent fifteen years arguing that it was an escape from the financial system.
>
> It won that argument. And the prize for winning was being absorbed into the thing it was escaping
> from — priced by the same interest rates, held in the same portfolios, sold in the same risk-off
